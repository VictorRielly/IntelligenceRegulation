# When EU AI Act marking and disclosure apply

**A16, checked 25 November 2026.** A11's two dates hold. The 2 December transition covers one provider duty for systems already on the market. A deployer deciding whether to label a deep fake or public-interest text needs the 2 August rule. [A11 handoff](20261125-apps-evidence-rules.md) · [AI Act, Article 50](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50) · [Amending regulation, Official Journal](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)

| Who and what | Applicable date and evidence |
|---|---|
| Provider of a system that generates synthetic audio, images, video or text, including general-purpose systems | **2 August 2026** under Article 113. Article 50(2) requires outputs to be “marked in a machine-readable format” and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. Technical feasibility qualifies the requirements for effectiveness, interoperability, robustness and reliability. The provision includes exceptions for assistance with standard editing, alterations that do not substantially change the input or its semantics, and specified uses authorised by law. [Article 50(2)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50) · [Article 113](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-113?full=1) |
| Provider of such a system placed on the market **before** 2 August 2026 | **By 2 December 2026** for Article 50(2). Article 1(39)(b) of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 adds Article 111(4), using the words “placed on the market before 2 August 2026”. The amendment was adopted on 8 July and published on 24 July. Article 4 gives entry into force on the third day after publication, **27 July** (derived from the publication date). [Official Journal, points 39(b) and Article 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng) |
| Deployer disclosing a deep fake or AI-generated or manipulated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest | **2 August 2026**. Article 50(4) provides for an appropriate disclosure in evidently artistic or similar works. It exempts qualifying public-interest text where human review or editorial control took place **and** a person or organisation holds editorial responsibility. The paragraph also specifies an exception for certain uses authorised by law. Under Article 50(5), affected people receive clear, distinguishable information by their first interaction or exposure, with applicable accessibility requirements. [Article 50(4)–(5)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50) |

**The Commission guidance resolves a publication-date edge case.** The Commission published the English guidance on 20 July. Paragraph 153 discusses existing systems placed on the market **or put into service** and limits the transition to marking and detection. For a system that is both interactive and generative, its interaction disclosure still starts in August. Paragraph 154 explains that earlier deep fakes need no retrospective label. For public-interest text, both generation and publication must precede 2 August to obtain that treatment. Text generated in July and first published in August falls under the disclosure rule, subject to its exceptions. The statutory transition itself uses “placed on the market”. [July guidance and English download](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems) · [Official Journal communication published 12 November, paragraphs 153–154](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/caabe0cd-de39-11f1-ba80-01aa75ed71a1.0006.01/DOC_1)

**The code is final and voluntary.** It was published on 10 June 2026. The Commission issued its positive assessment on 8 July and published the opinion on 9 July. The Commission and AI Board recognise the code as a means to demonstrate compliance. Section 1 addresses marking and detection by providers. Section 2 addresses disclosures by deployers. The underlying duties apply to covered actors regardless of signature. These primary records fill the code-status gap in A11. [Commission assessment](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-opinion-assessment-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content) · [Code and both sections](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content)

**Product test.** Submit a generated public fixture to the installed moderation classifier. Give an independent reader the output file alone, then the signed classifier receipt. Test separately whether the reader can detect a machine-readable generation mark, see a disclosure at first exposure, and verify the moderation decision. The current receipt authenticates only the third result. A marking product needs to bind a specific output to a marking operation and make the mark detectable from that output. A deployer must also present any required disclosure. A signed log or proof of a check, on its own, supplies neither user-facing behavior.
