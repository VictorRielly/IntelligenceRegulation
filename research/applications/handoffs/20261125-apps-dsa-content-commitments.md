# A20. Keep the per-item receipt off the public DSA database

**Finding.** Do not submit a hash or cryptographic commitment to individual moderated content with a statement of reasons. A public salt prevents reuse of precomputed guesses, but a reader who knows or can guess a post can still check it against the salted hash. A properly hiding commitment with a fresh secret opening can resist that test, but that does not make the attached record anonymous or give the database a field for it. Start with an ordinary database submission and a separate, access-controlled evidence record.

## What the authorities say

- **The rule covers the submission.** DSA Article 24(5) requires in-scope platforms to send the decisions and Article 17(1) statements of reasons without undue delay and to ensure the submitted information contains no personal data. The Commission's API requires a platform-unique statement identifier (PUID), and generates its own ID and UUID. The overview currently lists only EAN-13 for the structured content identifier. A syntactically valid PUID or free-text field does not authorize a content-derived identifier. A PUID is a statement identifier, not a user or content ID. [DSA Article 24](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065) · [Commission schema](https://transparency.dsa.ec.europa.eu/page/api-documentation) · [Commission field definitions](https://transparency.dsa.ec.europa.eu/page/documentation)
- **The test is whether a natural person is reasonably identifiable.** GDPR Article 4(1), Article 4(5) and recital 26 distinguish personal data, pseudonymisation and anonymity. If the platform can connect a commitment to a person using a post, account record, opening or other information it holds, treat the internal record as personal data. A text hash can be matched with a cached copy, screenshot text or a post the observer already knows. Whether the match identifies a person depends on the surrounding information. The Article 29 Working Party explicitly distinguishes a public salt from a secret key and warns that salted hashes over predictable inputs can still be matched. [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng) · [WP29 Opinion 05/2014, §4.1](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/atoms/files/88197.pdf)
- **An external recipient's context matters, but public release is different from transfer to one constrained auditor.** In *EDPS v SRB* the Court allowed for the possibility that a recipient cannot identify the subjects, while the controller can. The judgment applied the EU-institutions regulation and assessed that case's collection notice from the controller's perspective. It did not decide the DSA question. In *IAB Europe* the Court applied the GDPR and found that a string could be personal data where someone had reasonable means to associate it with identifiable users. The EDPB's 2026 anonymisation consultation draft, §§3.2 and 3.5.2, addresses access, linkage and inference by the relevant parties. This supports checking the combined release against likely auxiliary data, even when the cryptography hides the content bytes. [C-413/23 P, ¶¶52, 73–87, 99–112](https://juris.curia.europa.eu/juris/document/document_print.jsf?cid=2894705&docid=303863&doclang=EN&pageIndex=0) · [C-604/22, ¶¶43–51](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A62022CJ0604) · [EDPB Guidelines 02/2026, consultation version](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-022026-on-anonymisation_en)

## Design to test

| Candidate | What it adds to the public row | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain or stable hash of the item | A reusable test for known content and identical records | Do not submit. |
| Fresh public salt with each item hash | Stops matching equal content across different salts by digest alone. Anyone who sees the salt can still test a candidate for that row. | Do not submit. |
| Fresh hiding commitment with a secret opening | Can defeat direct public candidate matching. Metadata, reuse or subsequent opening can still make the row linkable. The platform may still be able to resolve it. | Keep it in the audit record. The EDPB draft illustrates commitments in a private verification flow, not as anonymous releases. [EDPB Guidelines 01/2025, annex Example 2 and n.39, consultation version](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2025-01/edpb_guidelines_202501_pseudonymisation_en.pdf) |
| Ordinary DSA row and separately controlled receipt | The documented statement fields. No receipt digest, content pointer or verifier link added. | Preferred. Use an opaque per-statement value for the required PUID, independent of item and account IDs, and keep the mapping internal. The normal row still needs a separate personal-data check. |

At decision time the provider would capture a versioned payload and bind its privately committed canonical input to the exact tokenizer, pinned model and policy. The provider keeps the receipt, a scoped linkage record and any opening legitimately needed for later verification. Separate access to openings from routine moderation logs. Set deletion and retention for each artifact under the applicable basis and purpose. Article 24(5) does not itself authorize retention of content or openings. A provider signature proves who signed, not the asserted decision time, unless the deployment adds an independent timestamp or a suitable witnessed log.

The proof would establish that the pinned model produced the claimed output on the committed input. It would not establish who wrote the content, whether the moderation action was correct or lawful, or that the committed input was the item actually reviewed absent a trustworthy capture binding. The prover sees plaintext. If content is later lawfully deleted, an already generated proof can still establish the computation relative to its retained commitment if retention is lawful. It cannot reconstruct the content or independently establish the linkage if the necessary evidence was also deleted. [Project privacy boundary](../../../docs/zkmatrix-poc/ZK60_PRIVACY_REQUIREMENTS.md)

**Confirm-or-kill test.** In a synthetic corpus with duplicates, short predictable items and one deliberately rare metadata combination, attempt to match exported records against independently available candidate posts and timestamps. Compare bare, publicly salted and secret-opening commitments with a normal row. Reject an export that reveals a content handle or enables a realistic person-level join. In the private verifier, require rejection after changing the captured input, pinned policy or signed linkage. Repeat validation after deleting the opening as the retention policy dictates and record which claims remain independently checkable. These are proposed tests, not regulator approval or a finding that any current proof profile already implements them.
