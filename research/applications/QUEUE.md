## Done

1. **A1, Mac capacity.** The installed app refuses 36–128 GiB under its current admission setting. Sequential one- and two-lane variants have estimated test targets of 48 and 64 GiB respectively. [Measurements and proposed confirmation](handoffs/20261124-apps-classifier-memory.md)
2. **A7, captured-image proofs.** A 12 MP RGB8 witness has 36 million input values. The existing canvas loses some full-resolution changes. End-to-end camera provenance needs a signed capture binding and an edit relation. [Measurements and prover estimates](handoffs/20261124-apps-captured-image.md)
3. **A2, open-weight text detectors.** GLTR needs one 124M decoder and per-token ranks. Binoculars detected 79.6% of unmodified RAID generations at 5% human false positives, falling to 43.5% with synonym substitutions. [Four detectors, costs and calibration test](handoffs/20261124-apps-text-detectors.md)
4. **A3, delegated proving.** The current operator can read submitted content. Confidential VMs and collaborative provers have measured costs on other workloads, and each requires a separate classifier qualification. [Trust assumptions, timings and deciding tests](handoffs/20261124-apps-delegated-proving.md)
5. **A4, private document checks.** An auditor can use a filter-execution receipt under a selected cross-domain control. The gateway must separately bind the attachment and filtering result to the transfer. Two open PII taggers need new proof relations. [Requirements, models, sizing and test](handoffs/20261124-apps-private-document-checks.md)
6. **A5, hidden benchmark scores.** A trained 8B receipt reaches one held-out next token. Binding every hidden question and gold answer to the published score is open. Extrapolating one trained CPU run to 1,000 prompts gives 135.4 serial proving hours. [Scoring relation, measurements and published precedents](handoffs/20261124-apps-model-evaluations.md)
7. **A6, writing provenance.** A receipt can authenticate a recorded edit history and the submitted text. A person retyping model output can satisfy the same typing policy as an unaided writer. [Existing recorders, proposed statement and deciding test](handoffs/20261124-apps-writing-provenance.md)
8. **A8, receipt size by workflow.** The guest post downloaded 2.009 GB and its three native checks consumed 128.19 seconds. B6a models about 1.00–1.06 GB for those proofs. Central audit, per-reader replay and aggregate score publication have different requirements. [Five applications and sizing assumptions](handoffs/20261124-apps-receipt-size.md)
9. **A9, video edits.** RGB8 at 30 frames/s gives 186.6 million input values/s at 1080p and 746.5 million at UHD 4K. Two reference calculations matched a two-second crop, redaction and frame-rate conversion. [Counts, published proof measurements and first test](handoffs/20261124-apps-video-edits.md)
10. **A10, safety classifiers.** Llama Guard 3 (1B, 8B) and Nemotron Safety Guard 8B v3 after an adapter merge match the existing block operations; Granite Guardian, Qwen3Guard and ShieldGemma each need new relations, and every generative guard needs a multi-token label relation. [Table of nine models, gaps and test](handoffs/20261125-apps-safety-classifiers.md)
11. **A11, rules asking for evidence.** In the DSA, AI Act, California and UK texts read, the entitled party is a regulator or auditor with data access (who can rerun an open model) or the public through aggregate reports; a receipt beats a signed log only where a third party must check a pinned verdict without the content, for example a statement of reasons. [Provisions, comparison and test](handoffs/20261125-apps-evidence-rules.md)
12. **A13, what a buyer needs from a label.** The DSA database and transparency templates make a category mandatory but do not require it to come from a classifier; a registered one-question policy per category carries a category with the installed verdict bit, so a multi-token label is needed only for one-pass choice among categories, and the cost of n policy runs is unmeasured. [Formats, severity tiers, derivation and test](handoffs/20261125-apps-label-need.md)
13. **A15, transparency-database fields.** The DSA database has required `automated_detection` (Yes/No) and `automated_decision` (fully, partially, not automated) fields but none naming a model, policy or evidence, and no field for a receipt; a reference could only sit in the 5,000-character free-text `decision_facts`, which nothing checks. The database holds no content and no personal data; only a bounded six-month retention rule (terrorist content) was confirmed for the "nobody can rerun" case. [Fields, free-text capacity and test](handoffs/20261125-apps-dsa-database-fields.md)
14. **A16, AI Act marking dates.** Article 111(4) gives providers of existing generative systems until 2 December 2026 for marking. Deployer disclosure starts 2 August. The Commission and AI Board recognised the voluntary code. [Official Journal, guidelines and code](handoffs/20261125-apps-ai-marking-dates.md)
15. **A12, small-proof destinations.** The checked 669,561,472-byte classifier receipt needs at least 1,594 Ethereum transactions under the Fusaka calldata rules. A 1 KiB proof fits one transaction. Browser and phone checking still require verification-key, memory and timing measurements. [Platform limits and inference benchmarks](handoffs/20261125-apps-proof-size-limits.md)
16. **A18, cost of n policy runs against a multi-token label.** n separate runs cost n x 2.56 to 2.72 s of six-lane stream capacity and n x 669,561,472 bytes (n = 16: 41.0 to 43.5 s, 10.7 GB); the records support no shared-prefix saving; a k-token label fits the padded window for most item lengths (the design's one-pass route, not built), repeated runs would not prove the chain, and no time exists for any label stage at the installed profile. [Counts, room in the window and calibration effects](handoffs/20261125-apps-label-run-cost.md)
17. **A21, restricted n-way head.** At Shieldstral 3B T128 R13, the 16-label bank fits the 4,096-column width but adds 1,098,720 modeled OUT-C bytes. The retained two-way frame is 3,292,120 bytes, not the 7,161,056-byte draw count. [Counts and reproducible model](handoffs/20261125-apps-restricted-head.md)
18. **A20, DSA content commitments.** A public salt does not stop matching a known item. Keep the per-item commitment and opening off the submission, treat linkable internal evidence as personal data, and test the ordinary public row for joins. [Authorities, proposed design and test](handoffs/20261125-apps-dsa-content-commitments.md)
19. **A19, moderation retention.** The DSA six months is a complaint window, while Regulation 2021/784 Art. 6 requires covered terrorist-content preservation. Regulation 2026/1881 resumed a narrower private-messaging derogation on 31 July after the old one expired, with a 12-month cap, not a mandate. [Official Journal findings and receipt limits](handoffs/20261125-apps-dsa-content-retention.md)
20. **A14, guard policy-prompt lengths.** Counted with the publishers' tokenizer files: Qwen3Guard's default template is 297 tokens (337 with a 40-token input), so it fits neither T = 128 nor 256; Granite Guardian 3.3's is 116 (156), leaving 12 content tokens at T = 128 but 140 at 256; Llama Guard 3's tokenizer is gated, so its count is NOT VERIFIED (proxy estimate only). Qwen3Guard's first generated token is `Safety`, not the verdict, and Granite's is `<`; the installed code allows only the suffix `[4]`. [Counts, template fills, hashes and test](handoffs/20261126-apps-guard-policy-lengths.md)
