## 0. Headline

1. **What it is.** A CUDA implementation of HyperPlonk (PIOP) plus Brakedown (PCS, Spielman code, Merkle) in which two primitives, a vector inner product and a vector-by-scalar product over the extension field, are written as UINT8 matrix products with INT32 accumulation (8-bit limbs, lazy reduction once per chunk of at most 33,025 elements, paper eq. 23), and the Spielman encoder is written as a gathered batched inner product with an L2-resident panel schedule. Merkle hashing stays on ordinary cores (BatchZK's code). **H100, BabyBear (31-bit) degree-8 extension, 32 B per element** (section 7.1) [paper].
2. **Timings at 2^25** [paper]: inner product 0.85 ms, scalar-vector product 0.91 ms, degree-2 sumcheck 4.04 ms, encoder 11.58 ms; end to end 215.28 ms for 2^25 multiplication gates, 955x over a single-threaded CPU stack (DeepFold HyperPlonk plus Orion) and 36.2x over a GPU stack of Icicle sumcheck plus BatchZK encoder.
3. **Zero knowledge: none.** The text defines zero knowledge once (sections 1.2 and 2.2), never blinds, masks or pads anything, reports no hiding cost, and says it "preserves the soundness and security properties" of HyperPlonk and Brakedown (ethics section). Nothing here bears on the project's hidden-activation requirement; the P1 privacy rows (masks, pad columns) are not exercised [paper, read in full; grep for blind, mask, hiding returns only the producer-consumer wording].
4. **It is not a sound form of G3, and its evidence for G1 is narrower than G1's premise.** Sections 2 and 3 below. Short version: the primitives sit at 70 to 80% of the H100's memory bandwidth (DERIVED), so what the tensor cores bought was the removal of a compute limit on a kernel that was then memory-bound; the project's slot kernels are mostly not of the matrix shape; and the authors themselves left the dense GEMM-code route (G3's premise) unused.

## 2. Reading the numbers (DERIVED, script)

- **The primitives are memory-bound.** At 32 B per element the inner product moves 2.15 GB in 0.85 ms = 2.53 TB/s, 75% of an H100's 3.35 TB/s peak (ASSUMED spec); the scalar-vector product 70%; the degree-2 sumcheck 79% if the fold re-reads as Algorithm 1 is written (eval phase then fold phase), 48% if it were fused. So the measured 10.0x to 13.1x over their SIMT kernels (and the 88x over Icicle, whose absolute time is about 355 ms for the same sumcheck, which is a weak baseline, DERIVED from 4.04 x 88) is a **compute-bound-to-bandwidth-bound move**, not an open-ended tensor throughput gain. The limit that remains is bytes.
- **Degree matters and is not tensorized.** The paper's degree-3 path multiplies two tables elementwise on ordinary cores (5.1). The project's zero-check slots have 19 to 36 tables at degree 4 to 5 (tick 15, COUNTED there); K1 (MEASURED, `results/zk60_k0k1_roofline_20261118/README.md`) puts loads at 11 to 19% of the evaluation and about three quarters in the polynomial body, a product of per-table line values, not an inner product. **TensorZKP supplies no mechanism for that body.**
- **Limb cost for this project's field.** Byte-MACs per base-field product are 4 x 4 = 16 for BabyBear and 8 x 8 = 64 for Goldilocks; an Fp2 Goldilocks inner product term is (2 x 8)^2 = 256 byte-MACs against 4 schoolbook (3 Karatsuba) field products; element size is 16 B, the same as the project's native Fp2 word (DERIVED). The G1 note's "49.0x to 196.1x" compute-roof gain uses 1,500 TOPS against 0.85 T field products per second ([MODEL] there): **if the 1,500 TOPS counts two operations per multiply-accumulate (the usual vendor convention, not checked here), the ratios halve to 24.5x to 98x at 36x and 9x inflation, and to 13.8x at the schoolbook 64x** (script). In either case the roof is above the memory floor, which is where TensorZKP's own kernels stop.
- **The Metal path differs from the one measured.** `simdgroup_matrix` element types are half, float and bfloat (spec and Halide and WebGPU sources via search; no integer type found; Metal 4 tensors list Int8, M3 support unclear). An exact byte product means half inputs 0..255 and float accumulators exact to 2^24: **N <= 258 terms per accumulator, not 33,025** (DERIVED), so the lazy-reduction chunk is 128x shorter; the 16 x 16 grid combine per chunk is of the order of 1% of the field products it replaces (ASSUMED 25 instructions per product), not fatal. The RTX 5090 has the UINT8 path; by bandwidth alone (ASSUMED 1.8 against 3.35 TB/s) its primitives would be about 1.9x slower than the paper's times.

## 3. Is it the sound form of G1 and G3?

**G1 (INT8 slabs for round 0/1 sumcheck and Gram folding, bit-identical transcript).** The paper keeps the protocol unchanged (proof size and verifier time "identical" to the CPU stack, 7.1), so it fits G1's "no verifier change". But:
- It evidences only two-table inner products and scalar folds. The project's round-0 kernel is 30% of slot 18 and 25% of slot 14 (MEASURED, slot breakdown, 6.377 and 3.546 ms) and has a 19-table or 36-table body, not an inner product.
- Where the project does have a two-table product (the matrix-product sumchecks), I did not find which of the 12 device slots they are or how many GPU ms they hold in the records I read. If they are cheap, G1 has no large target. **Not located.**
- Its measured gain is bounded by bytes (section 2); the project's fold already runs at the copy roofline on the M3 Ultra (K0, MEASURED, about 700 GB/s), so the fold part of G1 has no headroom on the Mac and at most the compute-to-bandwidth move on the 5090.

**G3 (dense GEMM Reed-Solomon PCS with wide rows, ">100x more MACs").** The paper argues the other way. It names the matrix-NTT route of TensorFHE (O(N sqrt N)) as trading asymptotic complexity for Tensor-Core fit and keeps the **linear-time sparse code**, accepting a gather-based kernel (section 1.2). It does not test a dense Reed-Solomon GEMM encoder, so there is **no published measurement here for G3's premise**. Adopting its encoder would mean changing the code to Spielman (a protocol change: different distance, number of queries and opening size, none of which the paper gives) and re-proving in Lean, for an encode share I could not split out locally: commit_A plus commit_H are 23.8% of block GPU at W16R2_8B and 30.6% at Shieldstral (MEASURED, slot breakdown README), but NTT versus Merkle hashing inside them is not in the records I read. Upper bound if encoding were free is 1/(1 - f) with f at most 0.238 or 0.306 (x1.31 or x1.44), which no real kernel reaches; the A27 radix-4 NTT already exists.

## 4. Honest effect and a falsifiable test

- **Committed words per request:** none for G1 (no protocol change). A Spielman-style encoder would change opening bytes, which the paper does not report; unmeasured.
- **Proved tokens per second:** no support from this paper for a gain on the shipped zero-check kernels (x1.00 on the evidence); the stream is paced by GPU work in the zero-check slots (54.0% of block GPU at 8B, MEASURED), whose body TensorZKP does not tensorize. The compute-bound-to-bandwidth move is real for two-table sums on a 5090 but its size here is unmeasured: **no number proposed**.
- **Test T1 (smallest, for the coordinator to schedule on the GPU; I run nothing).** Bench one Fp2 Goldilocks two-table inner product over 2^24 elements on the Metal device: (a) the shipped SIMT-style kernel, (b) a half-input / float-accumulate `simdgroup_matrix` byte-product with a 256-term chunk, and (c) a copy of the same bytes. Report GB/s and the ratio to (c). **Kill** G1 on the Mac if (a) is already at or above 70% of (c) (then at most 1.4x remains, and the 5090 question is the only one left). **Pass** to a second step (a round-0 Gram fold) if (a) is below 40% of (c) and (b) reaches 60%. Cost: one small kernel, no prover change. The same bench on the 5090 with INT8 MMA is the real test of the paper's claim for this field.
- **Test T2 (local reading, no GPU).** Item 36 below: which device slots are two-table degree-2 sums and what share of block GPU they hold. If under 5%, drop G1.

## 5. Risks and what was not verified

- Figures 3 to 5 not read as data; the artifact not downloaded (field, code parameters, blinding in code unchecked). Sections 4.1 and 7.1 disagree on the field; I used 7.1.
- H100 and 5090 bandwidth and the 1,500 TOPS convention are from memory or the local [MODEL] note. "Icicle is a weak baseline" is an inference (355 ms against a 2 to 4 ms memory floor), not a measurement. The encoder 4.37x is unexplained.
