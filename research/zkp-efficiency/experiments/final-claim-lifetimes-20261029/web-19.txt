L39@P3: Zero-knowledge proof is a cryptographic protocol enabling provers to convince verifiers of a
L40@P3: statement’s validity without disclosing any additional information beyond its truthfulness. It can be
L41@P3: used to address security and privacy challenges in diverse fields, such as artificial intelligence, data
L42@P3: analytics, and blockchain.
L43@P3: In this dissertation, we delve into the zero-knowledge proof, examining both its theoretical
L44@P3: foundations and real-world applications. In theory, we introduce Libra, Virgo and Virgo++, a series
L45@P3: of pioneering zero-knowledge protocols that boast optimal prover time, rapid verifier time, and
L46@P3: succinct proof size while maintaining exceptional efficiency in practical settings. In practice, we
L47@P3: explore the groundbreaking implementation of zero-knowledge proof in creating secure, trustless,
L48@P3: and permissionless cross-chain bridges for blockchain networks. Furthermore, we also investigate
L49@P3: the application of zero-knowledge proof in the realm of machine learning, showcasing its potential
L50@P3: to ensure the integrity of machine learning models, as demonstrated with the decision tree model.
L51@P3: The applied zero-knowledge proof protocols presented in this dissertation offer robust security
L52@P3-4: assurances coupled with pragmatic efficiency.i

L237@P11: Introduction
L238@P11: The rapid growth of artificial intelligence, data analytics, and blockchains has resulted in an explosion
L239@P11: of sensitive data, including personal information, that requires secure and simple privacy-preserving
L240@P11: solutions. Unfortunately, data breaches have become increasingly frequent, resulting in significant
L241@P11: economic losses and serious concerns about data privacy and security. Cryptographic algorithms
L242@P11: and tools have been developed to address these challenges, and Zero-Knowledge Proofs [GMR]
L243@P11: (ZKP) has emerged as a promising solution. ZKP is a cryptographic protocol that enables a prover
L244@P11: to convince a verifier of the validity of a statement without disclosing any additional information
L245@P11: beyond its truthfulness. For example, the prover can convince the verifier that there exists a x
L246@P11: satisfying f(x) = y for a public function f without revealing x to the verifier. However, applying
L247@P11: ZKP to real-world use cases has been challenging due to the gap between its theoretical feasibility
L248@P11: and practical efficiency, especially for large-scale statements. Therefore, this thesis aims to advance
L249@P11: ZKP from both theoretical and application perspectives, bridging the gap. On the theory side, we
L250@P11: propose novel ZKP protocols with optimal prover time, succinct proof size, and succinct verifier time,
L251@P11: resolving a long-standing open problem. On the application side, we are the first to employ ZKP in
L252@P11: machine learning, providing practical guarantees for the integrity and reproducibility of machine
L253@P11: learning models. We also propose the first solution for building truly trustless and permissionless
L254@P11: cross-chain bridges in blockchains using ZKP, called zkBridge. Our applied ZKP protocols provide
L255@P11: rigorous security guarantees along with practical efficiency. The zkBridge technology has generated
L256@P11: excitement in the industry, with numerous entities and researchers joining the zkBridge collective
L257@P11: to bring the technology to wide industry adoption, creating a secure, universal foundation for
L258@P11: multichain interoperability.
L259@P11: 1.1 ZKP protocols with optimal prover computation
L260@P11: The area of ZKP has a rich history and has transitioned from a purely theoretical question to
L261@P11: solutions deployed at sophisticated cryptocurrency constructions, e.g., ZCash [Ben+14]. However,
L262@P11: the existing protocols cannot scale to complicated statements in practice because of heavy prover
L263@P11-12: computations. In particular, the ZKP protocol used in ZCash needs a prover running quasi-linear1.1. ZKP PROTOCOLS WITH OPTIMAL PROVER COMPUTATION 2
L264@P12: time in the statement size and each statement needs a separate trusted setup. They are both
L265@P12: time-consuming. To address the bottleneck of the prover time in ZKP, we introduce three protocols,
L266@P12: namely Libra [XZZPS19a], Virgo [ZXZS], and Virgo++ [Zha+20]. All these protocols focus on
L267@P12: accelerating proof generation in ZKP. We also develop libraries of these protocols and released
L268@P12: codes online [Liba; Vira; Virb] for developers.
L269@P12: 1.1.1 Libra
L270@P12: Libra presents a groundbreaking solution to the long-standing open problem of creating a ZKP
L271@P12: system based on layered arithmetic circuits that achieves optimal prover time, succinct proof size, and
L272@P12: succinct verifier time. Specifically, for a circuit of size C, Libra’s performance is as follows: (i) the
L273@P12: prover time is O(C), regardless of the circuit type; (ii) the proof size and verification time are both
L274@P12: O(d log C) for d-depth log-space uniform circuits (e.g., RAM programs). Moreover, Libra features
L275@P12: a one-time trusted setup that depends solely on the size of the input to the circuit and not on the
L276@P12: circuit logic. The foundation of Libra is a new linear-time algorithm for the prover of the interactive
L277@P12: proof protocol by Goldwasser, Kalai, and Rothblum (also known as the GKR protocol), as well as
L278@P12: an efficient method for transforming the GKR protocol into zero-knowledge using small masking
L279@P12: polynomials. Libra not only boasts impressive asymptotic performance but also demonstrates
L280@P12: practical efficiency. For instance, our implementation reveals that it takes just 200 seconds to
L281@P12: generate a proof for constructing a SHA2-based Merkle tree root with 256 leaves, outperforming all
L282@P12: existing ZKP systems. Libra’s proof size and verification time are equally competitive.
L283@P12: 1.1.2 Virgo
L284@P12: Libra requires a one-time trusted setup, which we aim to eliminate by designing a transparent
L285@P12: polynomial commitment (PC) scheme with succinct proof size and fast verification cost. Leveraging
L286@P12: this PC scheme, we develop Virgo, a transparent ZKP protocol that offers significantly improved
