## 1. In plain words, for Victor

**The empty theorem is fixed, and this time the fix is shown to be real.** The audit found that the strongest run
theorem, applied the way lane L34 said, was empty: one of its inputs (the contract of stage IN-C, the input stage)
could only be met by a stage that rejects every proof. The cause was a missing guard in one Lean contract, not in the
protocol. The guard is added (the same guard the blocks' contract already had). Then, because five earlier results
turned out weaker than claimed, this lane PROVED that the repaired contracts are met by the REAL stage verifiers (the
Lean copies of the code's verifiers that earlier lanes checked against the code), with no assumption beyond the tier's
numbers; that the honest proofs those verifiers accept fall inside the guard; and that the run's whole schedule can now
be built at the real stages, with only the blocks' inputs left. The audit's own Lean proof of emptiness no longer
compiles against the repaired tree.

**The two "parses" of the transcript now agree where it matters.** Per-block results were proved with the block's own
bookkeeping of where each Fiat-Shamir step ends; the run uses the run's bookkeeping. This lane proved that a
sub-protocol reads that bookkeeping only inside its own window, and that the two agree on every block's windows. So
per-block results about the openings and the mask check carry to the run.

**The soundness covers: restated, not yet proved.** The heart of soundness is four statements ("covers") saying, in
effect, "if the extracted data is wrong, some check started from a false claim". They are still assumptions. This lane
made them weaker and clearer: (1) a part's wrong output may now be caught in ANY part (the next part opens it), as the
audit asked; (2) the one big "alive" assumption is split into nine, one per kind of check, each needed only when the
checks before it are sound. The top theorem is restated with these, so every remaining assumption has its own name.

**One thing in the plan does not fit (finding L39-2).** The plan was to plug the proved single-block results (L31,
L36) into the run's block cover. They cannot be plugged in as they are: they are probability bounds for one block run
on its own, while the run theorem counts all checks of the whole run once and needs a non-probabilistic block cover.
What the block cover needs is the block's case analysis on the run's transcript (lane L27's `block_doom_r13`, with
four links), with L36's algebra for two of the links and this lane's parse congruence for the openings. The block's
"endpoint term" (`εend`, GA-3) is not a new term at the run level: the boundary openings are already counted in the
run's opening kinds (proved by L34); what is left of it is one deterministic link of the block cover.

**What is not done.** The covers, the input endpoint's three links (`hread`, `hBal`, `hdzero`), and everything the
audit listed outside the covers (zero knowledge, completeness, the real Shieldstral instance).

every kind once over the whole run's transcript and takes `hdblk` as a DETERMINISTIC statement on that transcript
("`BlockOn` fails ⇒ some doomed entrant"). A union of 26 per-block bounds would also exceed the target (26 × `εsTarget`)
[ARGUED]. What `hdblk` needs is the block's case analysis on the run's transcript (L27's `block_doom_r13`) with the

as covering the run's blocks.

seven runs are `M`'s three and `A`'s and `H`'s two each; the part's closing opening (`X`: the boundary a block opens)

OUT-C's verdict changes neither its layout nor its draws (`rc_outcLay_verdict`, `rc_outcV_draws`), so the contracts
