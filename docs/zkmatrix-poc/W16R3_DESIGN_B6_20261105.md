## 0. In brief

**Recommendation.**
1. **B6 does not go into W16R3.** It cannot be ready at the freeze:
   - no reviewed soundness argument exists yet for the tower on our coset hiding code;
   - its prover cost is unmeasured;
   - the version that meets the plan's < 50 MB target needs a second design round (row width, compact masks, one
     boundary commitment).
   This follows D67: B6 must not hold W16R3 up.
2. **W16R3 keeps B6 open, and B1 already helps it.** B1's coefficient rows make every generator row a product of
   per-bit factors, so the verifier's check of the code switch costs O(t log n), not O(t·n) (§8).
3. **Next profile: B6 in two steps, the second decided by a measurement.**
   - **B6a, "switch the clear vectors".** Each opening's clear vectors are committed into one small matrix `W` per
     block instead of being sent. A FOLD / code-switch / BASE tower then proves their checks. The commitments, the
     stage-1 column test and D59's independent row weights are unchanged.
     - Receipt: 387 MB (−53%).
     - Verifier: about ×5–15 faster on its PCS work.
     - Prover: within about ±5%.
     - Hiding: with today's site masks kept, its hiding follows from the merged `HidingOpening` lemmas by
       post-processing (§5).
   - **B6b, wide rows.** B6a, plus rows of 2^18–2^20 entries, compact site masks, A and H folded together, and one
     commitment for all boundaries. Only B6b reaches < 50 MB: 40 MB at 2^20, 43 MB at 2^18 with batched Merkle paths.
     - Cost: +13 to +42 ms of GPU per block on the Mac [ESTIMATE], up to ×1.36 on a GPU-bound forks phase.
     - It needs the tower's own zero knowledge.
     - Build it only if the half-day measurement of §9.3 keeps its prover cost under the kill rule.

**The biggest risks:**
- **B6 changes the PCS, which every soundness and hiding instance depends on.** It is the largest Lean change of

## 1. How fold-and-hop works in FlashProve, and how it maps onto our PCS

### 1.1 The mechanism, from its code

FlashProve's code does not use the words "fold-and-hop". Our plan's name for one **FOLD + code switch (CS)** stage
is FlashProve's "tower": "folds and code switches, down to a base case" (`lean/GLOSSARY.md:1041`). The proximity test
is four blocks (`blocks/iop/iopp.h:6-23`):

```
BAT     batches all open claims on the group and the claims on the out-of-domain samples ... into one claim
for each stage but the last:
  FOLD  folds 2^k main and 2^h mask rows into three single rows (the third from a fresh mask)
  CS    code switch: re-encodes the three rows as one new oracle message, which is the next stage
BASE    the last stage, one main row and one mask row, is sent, masked
```

**The blocks, one at a time:**
- **BAT.** All linear claims on a group are combined into one claim `<c, m> + <d, mu> = target`. The coefficients
  are powers of one challenge (`PUBLISHED-WORKS.md:237`: "here they are the powers of one challenge").
- **FOLD** is a zero-knowledge sumcheck over the row bits (`blocks/iop/fold.h:6-22`; `fold_rounds.h:9-21`):
  - The summand is `H(X) = U(X) + ε·(<c(X), m(X)> + …)`.
  - The rows are folded at X; U is a fresh hiding polynomial with 2k + 1 coefficients, committed in a fresh mask row
    before ε is drawn (`fold_algebra.h:81`).
  - Each round sends two coefficients. The verifier tests no equation here.
  - The output is one linear claim on three single rows: the main rows folded at the challenges, the old mask row and
    the fresh mask.
- **CS** (`blocks/iop/cs.h:6-25`):
  - **The new oracle.** The prover commits a new oracle g whose message holds the old rows' messages AND their random
    tails: "so an old row's symbol at a position x is a public linear form of the new messages".
  - **The positions.** The verifier reads the old rows at t random positions. Each symbol read becomes a *generator
    claim* on g, and a later stage proves it.
  - **The soundness argument** is `cs.h:322-362`. It ends "This argument is our own; no one has refereed it."
  - **The error** of the positions' round is (1 − distance)^t, plus (claims − 1)/|E| per list member
    (`cs.h:628-633`).
  - **Hiding** rests on fresh uniform tails at g's later openings, a uniform pad, and old tails long enough for the t
    positions (`cs.h:634-636`).
- **BASE** (`blocks/iop/base_case.h:5-18`):
  - The prover commits a masking oracle g* with uniform M\*, R\*.
  - After a challenge λ, it sends `M' = M* + λM` and `R' = R* + λR`.
  - The verifier checks the claim and the encoding at a list of positions.

**The codes are coefficient-form Reed–Solomon on cosets** (`blocks/code/code.h:6-24`):
- a symbol is `z^r Σ message_j z^j + Σ tail_j z^j`;
- each basis function is a product of per-bit factors `z^(2^i)`;
- so a generator row folds in O(log n).

**The sizing rules** (`iop/iopp.h:69-81`):
- the folded main message fills the next main rows exactly;
- the next mask rows take the two folded masks, every random tail and the pads;
- the random tails must hide every position drawn at a stage.

**Masks may "ride"** in the switch's prover message and share its Merkle tree, because they are made from private
randomness only (`iopp.h:47-56`).

**The proximity regime.** FlashProve works at the **Johnson radius**:
- the setting is `blocks/numbers/settings/full-goldilocks.txt:1`;
- out-of-domain samples pin one codeword in the list (`docs/protocol.md:101`);
- list-decoding counts rest on "the theorem on codes", which its accounting "takes as given … with no bits to spare"
  (`protocol.md:119`).

It also counts 20–32 bits of proof of work on every draw of positions (`full-goldilocks.txt:13-14`;
`protocol.md:226-236`).

**The "hop" across segments.** "The prover folds each segment's commitments into one short commitment and carries it
into the next segment" (`protocol.md:161`). In the full-size setting each of 25 segments:
- folds its group (`full-goldilocks.txt:62`, `:82`);
- reads its three trees at one list of positions (`:84`);
- the next segment's tree P1 holds the "carried message" (`:35`).

One five-stage test runs at the end (`:87`).

**Its shapes.** Rows are long and few:
- `code long log_n=23 ell=2^22 r=218`: 2^22-entry messages at about rate 1/2, with 218 random entries
  (`full-goldilocks.txt:16`);
- `group seg … k=7`: 128 main rows (`:27`).

A proof is 18.78 MB on average, and the C++ verifier takes about 21 s on one thread (`guide/performance.md:21`,
`:23`). So FlashProve's own numbers do not show a fast verifier. Its verifier is dominated by other work.

### 1.2 The mapping onto our PCS

Our PCS (`zkp-core/src/pcs.rs:1-38`) is Ligero-style:
- **The clear vectors.** It sends one clear vector `v_g = ρ_gᵀ[M | pads]` (n + Q Fp2 entries) per column-weight
  group, plus a proximity vector with independent uniform weights on every row (`zk_rhos`, `pcs.rs:952-973`).
- **The columns.** It opens Q = 280 columns (`challenge_indices`, `:1053`).
- **The verifier** re-encodes each vector and compares it at those columns (`verify_zk_tagged`, `:1188-1279`).

The vectors are absorbed before the indices are drawn (`:1049-1053`).

| | FlashProve | ours today | B6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| field | Goldilocks, cubic E (24 B) | Goldilocks, Fp2 (16 B) | unchanged |
| hash | BLAKE2b | tagged BLAKE3 | unchanged |
| row encoding | coefficient form, random tail (`code.h`) | evaluation form, inverse NTT, pads via `(X^n − 1)·g` (`pcs.rs:674-693`) | B1's coefficient form (Sol §2); pads = FlashProve's tail |
| proximity regime | Johnson radius + OOD samples | δ/2, unique decoding (D59) | **δ/2 kept**: list size 1, no OOD samples (§4.3) |
| proof of work | 20–32 bits on positions | none | none |
| stage-1 queries | t ≈ 218 with work | Q = q_pad = 280 | 280 (B6a); 275 at rows of 2^20 (B6b) |
| what replaces the clear vectors | FOLD + CS: never sent | sent in the clear | B6a: committed in `W` (an Orion-style switch, `PUBLISHED-WORKS.md:239`); B6b: FOLD on A ∪ H |
| the column comparison | generator claims on the new oracle | re-encode and compare | generator claims on `W` |
| later stages | own codes, rates down to 2^-5 (`full-goldilocks.txt:87`) | — | rates 1/8–1/32, t 120–138 at the same per-list error |
| across segments | the folded row hops into the next segment's tree | D55: each block on its own fork | **no carry between blocks**: it would serialize the forks. Each fork ends its own tower; the join runs one tower for the IO commitments |

**Why B6a switches the vectors instead of folding A.**
- The clear vectors already exist. A8's row-sparse store fold computes them: A's 37 vectors touch only 2.26 rows'
  worth of coefficients (`results/zk60_effplan_20261105/README.md:358`).
- Committing them to W keeps stage 1 exactly as it is: the same transcript up to the vectors, D59's independent
  uniform weights, and the same column test.
- A FOLD over A's 2,501 rows would instead need the full coefficient table: about 84 M Fp2 entries per block, read
  once more (§3.2). That cost belongs to B6b, where rows are long and few.

**The new transcript order in a fork:**
1. `A`'s and `H`'s λ and ρ.
2. Compute the 47 vectors.
3. Commit `W` and absorb its root.
4. `A`'s and `H`'s indices; open the columns as today.
5. W's tower: BAT with coefficients `μ_g·θ_x`, drawn after the positions; FOLD over W's 6 row bits; CS; FOLD; BASE.

### 2.2 The receipt under B6 [MODEL]

**The scaling rule.**

```
receipt ≈ 32 × [ t1·(8 + 8·R1 + Σ_trees (32 + 32·(ℓ + 1))) + tower + M + slots ] + join
R1 = committed entries / 2^ℓ   (≈ 84 M entries per block at W16R2; 78 M data entries with B1–B3)
t1 = the least t with (1 − (e + 1)/N)^t ≤ 2^-113.93, q_pad = t, K = 2^ℓ + t, N = 2^(ℓ+1)
```

**The tower stages:**
- Each stage is opened at t_s from the same rule: today's 2^-113.93 per list.
- Masks ride in each stage's tree: +2 Fp2 rows per position.
- BASE sends `M'` and `R'` for two rows.
- The model (`tower`, `best_tower`) picks the cheapest of about 300 stage plans under the rule.
- t1 = 280 at ℓ = 14 reproduces W16R2's Q exactly (an assertion in the model).

### 3.3 Verifier

**Today's verifier** [MEASURED]: 2.31–2.84 s for a 32-block receipt on 6 threads, and 4.53 s block by block

## 4. Soundness, round by round, against 2^-100

### 4.1 The rounds

**Today** [MEASURED / proved; L3 findings Part 7 §V2, Part 9 §W2]:
- **the rate** is LogUp's α round, 2^-102.93;
- **the rest:** M ≤ 2^-106 (assumed); each regular opening 2^-113 (field) and 2^-113.93 (columns); sumcheck rounds
  ≤ 2^-125.68.
- **the zone (query-free floor)** is 155/2^110 = 2^-102.72 (`zone_budget_le`). Its parts: LogUp 136, M 16, four
  regular openings 2, slots 0.5.

**New rounds** [MODEL; `model.py` `soundness`]:

| Round | B6a | B6b, 2^18 | B6b, 2^20 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| tower BAT, coefficients `μ_g·θ_x` (two independent factors: 2/p²) | 2^-127 | 2^-127 | 2^-127 |
| FOLD's hiding combination ε, 1/p² | 2^-128 | 2^-128 | 2^-128 |
| FOLD sumcheck round, degree 2, 2/p² | 2^-127 | 2^-127 | 2^-127 |
| FOLD line, mutual correlated agreement N/p² (`mca_rsCode`) | **2^-111** (W, N = 2^17) | **2^-109** (stage 1, N = 2^19) | **2^-107** (N = 2^21) |
| CS / BASE positions, (1 − (e + 1)/N)^t | 2^-114.07 | 2^-114.0 | 2^-114.1 |
| BASE λ, 1/p² | 2^-128 | 2^-128 | 2^-128 |
| stage-1 openings | unchanged (2^-113, 2^-113.93) | replaced by the stage-1 fold (above) | the same |

**The rate stays 2^-102.93.** Every new round is at most 2^-107, so none becomes the maximum.

**The zone sum** [MODEL]:
- **B6a:** 162.6/2^110 = **2^-102.65**. The block's W tower adds about 3.5, and the join's tower about 4.5.
- **B6b at 2^18:** 206/2^110 = 2^-102.31.
- **B6b at 2^20:** 298/2^110 = 2^-101.78.

**What it says:**
- **All of them clear the approved 2^-100** (`εs(q) = q·2^-100 + 2^-100`).
- **B6b spends 0.4–0.9 bit of the floor's margin.** The fold-line term grows with N, and k rounds of it add.
- **The model's tower rates trade bytes for zone.** Lower rates shorten t but raise N.
- **A soundness-aware plan would cap each stage's N** at about 2^17. This costs about 2–5% more bytes [ESTIMATE].
- **A Lean instance would re-prove `zone_budget_le`** at the new constant, as B4 would have.

### 4.2 The chain length

- **What the tower adds:** about 20 rounds per block, plus t-position squeezes per stage.
- **Against Part 9's premise** `ℓW + 1 ≤ 2^75`, this is harmless.

### 4.3 The proximity regime: keep D59's δ/2

**B6a keeps it exactly.** Stage 1 is the same column test with the same independent uniform weights
(`rs_proximity_half`, `opening_sound_rs_half`).

**The tower's stages need two facts at δ/2:**
1. **A list of size 1** when 2e < d. FlashProve proves it as `ld_list_unique`
   (`lean/Cb/Ld/LdRelJohnsonProofs.lean:40`; `LdTowerThird.lean:28-30`: "2 e < d is enough").
2. **Mutual correlated agreement on lines,** with at most N bad values. This is BCIKS20 Thm 4.1. We already have it:
   `mca_rsCode` (`formal/ZK60Formal/ThirdParty/FlashProve/Code/Half.lean:135-140`, ported from FlashProve). Its
   statement: "If 2 e + K + 1 ≤ n … mutual correlated agreement on lines at the radius e with at most n bad values,
   for any number of interleaved rows".

**So B6 needs no list decoding and no out-of-domain samples.** Unique decoding pins the message, which is why
FlashProve's samples exist (`cs.h:322-348`).

**The Johnson radius is not recommended.** At rate 1/2 it would cut stage 1's t from 280 to about 230 [MODEL], or
−17% of the column bytes. But it rests on the list-decoding theorem that FlashProve takes as given and no Lean proof
covers. D59's direction ("no gaps in our proofs") rules it out.

**Proof of work** would also shorten t. Our Lean Fiat–Shamir model does not count grinding. So it is out too.

**B6b's stage-1 FOLD folds A ∪ H with tensor (eq) weights,** not D59's independent weights. Each round is a line, so
`mca_rsCode` applies round by round. But the conditions D59(1) checked have to be re-checked for this form, in a
written check like `handoffs/20261101-delta-half-lemma-check.md`:
- the coset domain;
- the pads;
- the interleaving;
- the Fp rows under Fp2 weights (`close_of_close_lift`).

B6b-CS (§3.2) avoids this check by keeping the independent weights.

## 5. Hiding

### 5.1 What is new in the view

**No longer sent:**
- the 43–47 clear vectors per block;
- the 97 at the join.

**New:**
- **the roots** of `W` and of each later stage;
- **FOLD's messages:** the sum of U, then two coefficients a round;
- **BASE:** the masking oracle's root, `target*`, `M'` and `R'`;
- **the openings:** the opened columns of each stage (with salts and paths), and the masks riding in them.

**Unchanged:**
- stage 1's opened columns (t = 280 ≤ q_pad = 280);
- the published slot terminals and their site masks.

### 5.2 B6a with today's site masks: no new zero-knowledge argument

**Today's clear vectors are uniform.** Today the verifier sees every clear vector. Each touches a reserved mask pair,
and the prox vector touches the prox pair (`pcs.rs:1006-1021`). So the view's law does not depend on the data
(`HidingOpening.openingView_law_eq`; `HidingOpeningSum`; `HidingBlock`).

**Everything B6a adds is computed from today's view and fresh coins:**
- W's message is those vectors plus fill;
- W's openings, FOLD's messages and BASE's values are computed from W, public challenges and fresh coins.

**So the joint view stays independent of the data by post-processing.** No new kind of argument is needed. W could
even be a plain commitment and the tower a plain IOPP. The toy confirms it:
- **P2:** B6a with today's masks hides;
- **I2:** it still hides without U or BASE's masking oracle;
- **I3:** it still hides with no W pads, no fill, no U and no masking oracle.

**What a simulator must produce:** today's simulator's output (the vectors, the stage-1 columns, the published
values). Then it runs the honest W and tower computations on it, with fresh coins.

**One condition.** The stage-1 positions are drawn after W's root instead of after the absorbed vectors. Under fixed
challenges that changes nothing. Under Fiat–Shamir it is a re-instantiation of the existing chain argument.

### 5.3 B6b's compact masks: the tower's masks become load-bearing

**Full-row site masks do not survive wide rows.** At 2^20-entry rows they would be 310 × 2^20 ≈ 325 M entries,
about 4× the data. B6b therefore replaces them by **compact slices**: two random entries per published value. This is
possible only because no clear vector is sent any more.

**What the switched data then needs.** It is no longer uniform. The toy shows that each of these holds the hiding,
and each control fails without it:
1. **Every committed stage covers its opened positions with random coefficients:** its pads plus uniform unused
   message entries. FlashProve's rule is the same: "the honest prover fills them … with uniform values in CS,
   because the argument for hiding … counts these entries as uniform" (`cs.h:80-85`).
   - **I1:** zero fill with t_W = q_pad_W still hides;
   - **N3:** zero fill with t_W = q_pad_W + 1 leaks;
   - **N2:** t_W beyond pads + fill leaks.
2. **FOLD's hiding polynomial U,** whose coefficients are committed before ε. FlashProve's Lean states the property:
   "the map from the coefficients to (the sum, the k round polynomials) is … a bijection onto the set of all
   consistent messages" (`lean/Cb/Ld/LdHide.lean:9-16`). Without it the toy leaks (N4).
3. **BASE's masking oracle,** uniform `M*`, `R*` (`base_case.h:14-18`). Without it the toy leaks (N5).
4. **Stage 1's q_pad ≥ t1,** unchanged. Without it the toy leaks (N6).
5. **The compact masks on the published values.** Without them the toy leaks (N7). A plain tower with compact masks
   leaks too (N8).

**Do our q_pad pads survive the folding? Yes, and they change role:**
- **In stage 1** they hide the t1 opened columns exactly as today: PG-R2's Vandermonde block, full rank on at most
  q_pad distinct points.
- **In a code switch,** a vector's pad part (`ρᵀ·pads`) becomes ordinary message entries of the next stage, so it is
  carried, not consumed. FlashProve says the same: "the old randomness becomes a part of the message, so that the old
  codeword is a linear function of the new message" (`lean/Cb/Ld/LdSwitch.lean:24-25`; `cs.h:12-15`).
- **In a FOLD,** the folded pads go into the next stage's mask rows (`iopp.h:75-78`).
- **Each stage needs its own fresh pads** for its own openings.

**What would have to be proved for B6b:**
- **The tower's HVZK at our codes, composed stage by stage.** FlashProve's statement: "honest-verifier zero
  knowledge: exact; against what follows reading at most t' long positions of the new word; the simulator is given at
  most D.t long positions of the input word" (`lean/Cb/Ld/LdTower.lean:54-56`).
- **The compact-mask lemma** in the style of `HidingRows`.
- **The run-level joint rank,** rerun over every stage.

### 5.4 The toy test [MODEL, `model.py` §5]

**The setting:**
- the field F_65537 (3 generates it; the coset is 3·G_N);
- B1's coefficient encoding with pads;
- stage 1: 2 witness rows, n 8, q_pad 3, N 16, t 3;
- W: n 16, q_pad 3, N 64, t 3;
- FOLD over 2 row bits;
- three challenge draws each.

**The criterion.** For fixed challenges every viewed value is a linear form in (witness, randomness). The view's law
is independent of the witness iff `rank(R) = rank([R | W])`, computed exactly.

| Configuration | Witness hidden | Honest verifier equations |
|---|---|---|
| P1 today's opening, full-row masks | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| N1 today's opening, compact masks | **0/3** | 3/3 |
| P2 B6a, today's masks | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| P3 B6a, compact masks, every tower mask | **3/3** | 3/3 |
| N2 t_W = 9 > pads + fill | 0/3 | 3/3 |
| I1 zero fill, t_W = q_pad_W | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| N3 zero fill, t_W = q_pad_W + 1 | 0/3 | 3/3 |
| N4 no U | 0/3 | 3/3 |
| N5 no masking oracle | 0/3 | 3/3 |
| N6 t_A = q_pad_A + 1 | 0/3 | 3/3 |
| N7 published values unmasked | 0/3 | 3/3 |
| I2 today's masks, no U, no masking oracle | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| I3 today's masks, a fully plain tower | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| N8 a plain tower, compact masks | 0/3 | 3/3 |

**What the toy does not show:**
- Merkle hiding;
- Fp2 challenges over Fp rows;
- sampling with replacement;
- more than one code-switch stage;
- the 32-block joint view.

It is a falsifier, not a proof.

## 6. Refusals (D66: challenge-only; D65(2): one constant string)

**B6 adds no refusal:**
- FOLD, CS and BASE contain no division by a challenge and no branch on the data.
- Repeated positions are opened once and counted twice. Indices are drawn with replacement, as
  `SoundColumnTest` models.
- A degenerate challenge (ε = 0, λ = 0, θ = 0) only weakens soundness by the counted 1/|E|. It never stops the
  prover.
- The B6 verifier's rejections are public checks.

**Two cautions:**
- **FlashProve's verifier "at some rare challenge values … exits without a verdict, or rejects, even on an honest
  proof"** (`protocol.md:240`). The B6 build must have no such case; a control should cover it.
- **A prover self-check that a mask was drawn** (as `base_case.h:19-27` does) is a fault check, not a refusal. If one
  fails, the run aborts with D65(2)'s single external string and nothing in the private log that depends on hidden
  data.

**Existing refusals are unchanged:** the class-(B) refusals before any commitment (`refuse_lookup_misses`) and the
LogUp rules of D66.

## 8. Interactions

**B1, coefficient rows (W16R3; Sol's note §2).**
- B1 makes the generator row at a position the power vector `(z^i)` and `(z^n − 1)z^j`. Both are products of per-bit
  factors.
- So the verifier evaluates its folded form in O(t log n). Without B1 it costs t × (n + Q) per row group: about 4.7 M
  Fp2 products per block, or 10–20 ms on one thread.
- B6 keeps Sol's rule: the circuit's multilinear weights stay on d, and only the generator claims use polynomial
  weights.

**B2 and B3.**
- B2's fewer groups shrink W: 47 → 43 vectors.
- B3's fewer rows cut stage 1's columns by 4.9%.
- Neither conflicts.

**D55.**
- Each fork runs its own tower inside its own transcript, so block-local zones hold (Part 9).
- No folded row hops from block to block: that would serialize the forks.
- The join's IO openings share one `W_join` (B6a), or become one boundary commitment (B6b). The latter changes D55's
  phase 1 from 31 boundary roots to one, and so `FSTreeD55`'s trunk.

**A8 and A12.**
- A8's store fold still computes the vectors; they feed W's commit instead of the transcript. The 12.5 MB absorb that
  A8 left undone disappears.
- A12's resident device rows stay the source of the stage-1 column gathers.
- B6b's stage-1 FOLD would be a new device kernel over those resident rows.
- A8's timing note (the small-word path) is unchanged and is not B6's.

**A13 and A14.** A13 closed negatively and A14 merged (Sol's note §1). No conflict.

**B5 (LogUp-GKR).** If B5 ever retires H, stage 1's rows halve. It is compatible.

**M.** M is unchanged: rate 1/4, its own accounting, not covered by D59. At B6b it is 20% of the receipt.

## 9. Recommendation, risks and the experiments

### 9.1 The recommendation

1. **Not in W16R3.** B1 goes in as planned and serves B6 later.
2. **Next profile: B6a first.**
   - **Value:** half the receipt, and a verifier about ×5–15 faster on its PCS work.
   - **Cost:** no measurable prover cost [ESTIMATE ×0.99–1.05].
   - **Proofs:** no new zero-knowledge argument; a soundness proof assembled from merged lemmas plus the tower.
3. **B6b only if §9.3 allows.** It is the only way to the plan's < 50 MB (40 MB at 2^20). It is a new layout, a new
   hiding argument and a new D55 trunk.
   - **On the Mac** it is a net prover cost.
   - **On the RTX 5090,** where the host limits, it may hide.
   - **The decision** is Victor's, between receipt size and the D64 clock.

### 9.2 The open risks

**B6a:**
1. **The tower's soundness on our code is not yet written down or reviewed.** FlashProve's own switch argument is
   unrefereed (`cs.h:362`).
2. **The transcript reorder:** both openings' λ/ρ come before W, and both openings' indices after it. It needs D17's
   new profile string, and `FSOpening`'s chain changes.
3. **The receipt stays near 390 MB.** Some deployment uses would still find that too large.

**B6b:**
1. **Prover cost:** +13 to +42 ms of GPU per block [ESTIMATE].
2. **Compact masks:** hiding then rests on the tower. Only the toy test exists.
3. **The stage-1 fold's eq weights** need a D59-style check (§4.3).
4. **The group count at wide rows:** more planes become sub-row slices. This changes B6b-CS's W size, so it must be
   recounted from `a_groups`.
5. **The zone margin falls by 0.4–0.9 bit.**
6. **One boundary commitment** changes D55's reviewed phase 1.

### 9.3 What would falsify the model, and the cheapest experiments

**Already done here, at no cost:**
- the census;
- the byte model;
- the toy joint-view test with its controls.

**The cheapest next experiment** (about half a day): one ignored test in the gpu-metal tree, under the resource guard.
It uses existing functions only. Its timings:
- `pcs::commit_zk_owned` at W's shape: 88 Fp rows × 2^14 at inv_rate 8, and the same at inv_rate 2;
- A's 2,279 data rows committed at log_cols 14, 18 (143 rows) and 20 (36 rows), the same data. This is B6b's NTT
  growth;
- BLAKE3 absorb of 12.5 MB on one thread: B6a's saving;
- a CPU stand-alone of the dense-coefficient row-fold sumcheck at 84 M entries. It gives the arithmetic of B6b's
  stage-1 FOLD before a Metal kernel is written.

**Kill rules:**
- **B6a** is not worth a profile version if its measured net prover cost exceeds +3% on the N = 6 forks phase
  (about +0.11 s). Its benefits are receipt size and verifier time only.
- **B6b** is deferred, leaving B6a only, if the 2^18 commit plus the fold stand-alone add more than +25 ms of GPU per
  block (about +15% of 0.165 s).
- **B6b's compact masks** are dropped if the toy test, extended, finds any rank defect not removed by the budgets of
  §5.3. The extended test needs:
  - two code-switch stages;
  - Fp2 challenges over Fp rows;
  - sampling with replacement.

  Without compact masks B6b is impossible at wide rows.
- **Soundness:** B6 stops if a written check of the per-round line lemma for our encoder does not reproduce N/|Fp2|
  per fold round. The check covers the pads, the coset, the interleaving and the Fp/Fp2 split, as D59(1) did.
