# The Aggregation ZK Gap — Plain-Language Explanation and the Options

Status: decision document, written 2026-07-27 at the U-AG3 close, for
Victor. Technical source: AGGREGATION_ZK_DESIGN.md (Sections 4.4-4.6
contain the proofs and prices; this document is the plain-language
companion and the standing menu of ways forward). Nothing here adds
new results; it organizes the recorded ones for a later decision.

## 1. Background: what "zero-knowledge" buys us, in this project

A proof of an LLM forward pass convinces a verifier that "this output
really came from running this model on this input" without the
verifier re-running the model. Two separate properties matter:

- **Soundness** — the prover cannot fake it. (Intact everywhere; not
  at issue here.)
- **Zero-knowledge (ZK)** — the proof itself reveals nothing about
  the private data behind it (model weights and/or user
  inputs/activations, depending on deployment). A transcript is
  zero-knowledge when everything the verifier sees could have been
  generated by a "simulator" that never saw the private data.

Whether ZK matters depends on the deployment. Proving "public model,
public input, public output" needs no hiding at all. Proving
"proprietary weights" or "private user prompt" needs real hiding.
The project has treated ZK as a hard requirement to date (the R1
masking tranche, the Z1 wave masking) because the deployment story
includes private-data settings.

## 2. What the aggregation redesign changed

The old (current-code) protocol produces gigabytes of proof per layer
(18.59 GiB at the FFN shape — PROOF_BYTE_LEDGER.md), but after the Z1
masking work it was fully hiding: every wire byte was provably
simulatable.

The Greyhound reroute (U-AG/U-AG2/U-AG3) collapses those gigabytes to
~256 KiB per layer by committing everything into a few global
commitments and proving one batched "evaluation claim" with a
recursive compression stack (Greyhound + LaBRADOR). U-AG3 confirmed
the bytes and the soundness both land. The ZK lane (Agent EC,
AGGREGATION_ZK_DESIGN.md) then found that TWO of the three places the
new protocol could leak are fully closed with exact simulators — but
the third is not closable with any known technique at our modulus.
That third place is "the floor."

## 3. The hard negative, spelled out

### 3.1 What the floor is

The LaBRADOR compression works by repeated folding: each level mixes
the (huge) witness down to a smaller one using public random
challenges, commits to the smaller one, and recurses. After 7-9
levels the witness is small enough that recursing further would cost
more than it saves — so the protocol STOPS and sends the final small
witness IN THE CLEAR, and the verifier checks it directly (checks its
algebra against the commitments, and checks that its entries are
small numbers — the "norm check"). That final cleartext object is
the recursion floor. At our derived parameters it is roughly
64,000-80,000 small integers per proof.

### 3.2 Why the floor is a leak

Each of those integers is a deterministic function of the private
data: fold-challenge combinations of the committed tensors (the
challenges are public, the tensors are the secret). In the simplest
(unrecursed) case the functions are LINEAR — so a verifier who
collects several proofs of the same witness under fresh challenges
can literally solve the linear system and recover the secret tensors
EXACTLY. This is not hypothetical: the lane's spike script
(scripts/aggregation_zk_spike.py, certificate SPKZ-6) performs the
recovery at toy scale — full rank, exact answer. With recursion the
functions become nonlinear (digit extraction between levels), which
makes recovery harder but is deliberately NOT credited as protection
(digit-structured integer systems are exactly what lattice decoders
are good at).

### 3.3 Why you cannot just mask it

The standard fix would be to add random noise ("masking") to the
floor before sending it. The floor is norm-checked — the verifier
must confirm its entries are small, because that is what the
soundness of the whole lattice argument rests on. That creates a
trap with no exit at our modulus (q ~ 2^31):

- A mask WIDE enough to statistically hide the values makes the
  masked object fail (or make meaningless) the norm check.
  Measured: the required mask width lands at ~2^32-2^37 against a
  norm line of q/8 = 2^28. Dead on norms.
- A mask NARROW enough to pass the norm check does not fully hide —
  bounded noise on bounded values leaks through range bias (there is
  no one-time pad on bounded integer supports).
- The computational escape (rejection sampling, the standard lattice
  signature trick) works arithmetically in one corner of parameter
  space — but it makes every wire number ~4x wider, which costs
  256-700 KB just for the floor response, versus roughly 80 KB for
  the whole bare floor and 262,144 B for the ENTIRE budget. Dead on
  bytes: an artifact with a masked floor is at least 1.77x budget.

### 3.4 Why you cannot restructure around it (the "digit wall")

Five sanitization routes ("never send sensitive material at all")
were each worked out and each fails (AGGREGATION_ZK_DESIGN 4.4):
opening a uniformized sum instead of the value; blinding at the
digit level; pad columns folded in; binding the blind by a hash;
MLWE-structured blinds. The common killer is structural: at q ~ 2^31
the norm budget forces every committed value to be chopped into
small DIGITS before commitment (digit decomposition). The commitment
then binds digits, not values. The digit map is nonlinear (carries),
so the commitment is NOT linear in the values — and every known
lattice zero-knowledge opening technique relies on exactly that
linearity (masked responses act on the linear structure; blinding
needs hom(x) + hom(b) = hom(x+b)). That is the digit wall: small
modulus => digits => no linearity => no known ZK opening. A bigger
modulus restores the linearity room but pays bytes everywhere else
(that trade is Option C below).

Honesty note: "no known technique" is a survey claim, not a theorem.
It is posed to the standing external review as a sharp question
(AZ-T1): does a zero-knowledge opening for balanced-digit Ajtai
commitments at q ~ 2^31 exist with response + proof under ~100 KB?

### 3.5 What is NOT leaked, and what "confined" means

Everything else in the new protocol is proven hiding: the sumcheck
rounds (masked, exact simulators), the terminal claimed values
(blinded by selector-extension pads), the commitments themselves
(hiding under the standard MLWE assumption). The simulator for the
whole transcript is complete EXCEPT for this one floor object; its
dimension and its accumulation law (how much more an adversary
learns per additional proof) are stated exactly. The interim
deployment rule "at most one aggregate proof per witness — re-proof
requests are served from cache" stops cross-proof accumulation
operationally.

## 4. The options (documented for a later decision)

The adopted interim posture is Option A. Options are not mutually
exclusive; B-E can each end the interim.

### Option A — Ship under a non-ZK label with the confinement rules
(ADOPTED INTERIM POSTURE, U-AG3 FINAL)

Continue building the aggregation stack; deploy it only where the
leak is acceptable; label it honestly (the M4/R1 house precedent:
non-ZK until the fix lands); enforce one-proof-per-witness; run the
AZB battery to certify the leak stays confined to the floor object.

- Upsides: zero bytes, zero new research, zero schedule cost — U-AG4
  (folding) and the build round proceed now. Fully honest: the label
  states exactly what leaks. Perfectly fine for public-data
  deployments (open-weight model, non-private inputs), which
  includes most benchmark/verification scenarios. The old fully-
  hiding wave path still exists in the codebase for anything else.
- Downsides: NOT zero-knowledge — ~64-80k witness functionals leak
  per proof; private-weight and private-input deployments are out of
  scope until an escape opens; the one-proof rule is an operational
  constraint someone must enforce; carrying a "non-ZK" label on the
  flagship artifact has optics/adoption cost.
- When it ends: whenever B, C, D, or E lands.

### Option B — Buy full ZK with bytes: raise the per-layer budget to
~450-500 KiB (escape E4)

At ~1.8-2x the current layer budget, the floor CAN be masked
(the rejection-sampled response fits), and with the exact-digit
configuration (already adopted) that closes every surface: full
zero-knowledge with only known, already-analyzed techniques.

- Upsides: the ONLY currently-actionable path to full ZK. No new
  cryptography — the arithmetic is done and spike-checked. Still
  ~40,000x smaller than today's 18.59 GiB/layer. Could be offered as
  a MODE (ZK mode at ~500 KiB, fast/small mode at ~256 KiB under
  Option A's label) rather than a global change.
- Downsides: breaks the frozen TARGET_CONTRACT 256 KiB line — a
  policy amendment, R1-discipline territory, not a quiet edit. The
  norm side sits AT the line only in the optimistic no-limb-growth
  corner (2^27.2-2^28.6 vs 2^28.00) — real engineering risk that the
  corner closes under implementation detail; needs a build-round
  feasibility check before being promised. Per-proof prover cost of
  mask sampling (small but nonzero).
- Trigger to revisit: at U-AG4 FINAL (once folding tells us the
  per-pass bytes, the pass-level headroom for a ZK mode is known).

#### Option B at the 8B target: how big do the proofs actually get?

Scope note first: every number below is for the 32-layer FEEDFORWARD
(FFN) chain — the project's current unit of work. Attention
(softmax/RMSNorm) is post-audit scope and not yet specced or priced;
a full-forward proof will be larger by that not-yet-designed part.
The reference FFN shape is 512x4096x11008 (7B-class dims); the
8B-class shape (4096x14336) changes these numbers only weakly,
because the evaluation proof is recursion-floor-dominated and the
sumcheck transcript grows logarithmically in tensor size [MODEL].

Per 32-layer FFN pass, WITHOUT cross-layer folding (i.e., today's
design state, before U-AG4):

| mode | per layer | x32 pass | vs today's measured 595 GiB/pass |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Option A (non-ZK label, exact-digit) | 283,392 B (276.8 KiB) | 9,068,544 B ~= 8.65 MiB | ~68,800x smaller |
| Option B (full-ZK mode, masked floor) | 463,616-529,152 B (~453-517 KiB) | 14.8-16.9 MB ~= 14.1-16.1 MiB | ~35,000-40,000x smaller |

Per pass WITH U-AG4 folding (the next design round; numbers here are
direction, not results): the ZK surcharge is paid PER EVALUATION
PROOF, not per layer — the floor is a property of each
Greyhound/LaBRADOR proof. If folding lands its target shape (the 32
layers' claims folded into ONE aggregated instance with one final
evaluation proof), the pass carries one floor, so the full-ZK
surcharge drops from 32x(+176-240 KB) ~= 5.6-7.7 MB to ONE
+176-240 KB. A folded full-ZK pass then plausibly lands in the
0.5-2 MiB class [MODEL — NOT yet derived; this is precisely U-AG4's
deliverable, and EA's floor analysis (even perfect descent/eval
folding leaves ~1.50 MB of per-layer instance transcripts unless the
instances themselves fold) is the obstacle it must clear]. This
per-proof-not-per-layer structure is the main reason the Option B
decision should wait for U-AG4's numbers: folding may make full ZK
nearly free at pass scale relative to the unfolded 14-16 MiB.

### Option C — Move the opening layer to a bigger modulus (q46+;
escape E1)

At q ~ 2^46 the digit decomposition relaxes enough that masking room
exists (the digit wall recedes). The house already owns q46
machinery (the gadget/range layer).

- Upsides: structural fix at the source; keeps response widths sane;
  reuses an existing house modulus.
- Downsides: measured 1.35x OVER budget purely from wider
  coefficients inflating every row (PARAMS 5.2 — q46 does not rescue
  bytes, it spends them); AND it requires a tensor-scale cross-
  modulus link, which the claim-reduction analysis currently forbids
  (CR 12.2 rule 2 — the dense-weight verifier boundary); that link
  is an open design problem of its own. Blocked twice today.
- Trigger to revisit: only if someone solves the tensor-scale link
  cheaply (would show up as a U-L-lineage design result).

### Option D — A new cryptographic primitive (escape E5; research)

Solve AZ-T1: a zero-knowledge opening for balanced-digit Ajtai
commitments at q ~ 2^31, response + proof <= ~100 KB.

- Upsides: would give full ZK at the CURRENT budget — the clean win.
  The question is now posed sharply enough to hand to the external
  review and to watch the literature for (O-9 watch item: the
  2026/1289 toolkit lineage at small moduli is the most likely
  source).
- Downsides: may simply not exist (the survey says nothing published
  comes close); timeline unknowable; cannot be planned on. This is a
  lottery ticket, not a roadmap item.
- Trigger: literature watch + the external reviewer's answer.

### Option E — Keep the fully-hiding wave architecture for
privacy-critical deployments (dual-track)

The pre-reroute protocol (current code, Z1 wave masking) is fully
HVZK at engine scale — proven, built, battery-tested. It remains
available as the privacy track while the aggregate track serves the
size-critical uses.

- Upsides: full ZK TODAY with zero new work; already hardened
  (Z1 batteries green); no label caveats.
- Downsides: gigabytes per layer (2.9-18.6 GiB scale) — unusable
  wherever the 256 KiB target matters, i.e. it surrenders the whole
  point of the aggregation trench for those deployments; two
  protocol tracks to maintain and audit forever; the succinctness
  wall was the original Phase 3 mandate.
- Realistic role: the interim answer for any genuinely
  private-weight demo while A-D play out, not a destination.

### Non-options, recorded so they are not re-litigated

- Masked floor response WITHIN the current budget (escapes E2/E3):
  priced dead on bytes (+176-620 KB vs a 262,144 B total); E4 is
  exactly this option done honestly (pay the bytes). See 4.4/4.5.
- Pretending the nonlinear (recursed) floor is safe "because it's
  complicated": explicitly rejected by the house honesty rule; no
  margin is credited to unanalyzed hardness.
- Silently re-exposing wave structure to dodge the floor: undoes Z1;
  forbidden by the standing constraint.

## 5. Recommendation on file (orchestrator, 2026-07-27)

Option A now (adopted at the U-AG3 FINAL, already in force), with
Option B formally evaluated at the U-AG4 FINAL as a ZK MODE decision
(the two-mode reading: ~256 KiB labeled / ~500 KiB full-ZK), Option
D standing as the external-review question and literature watch, and
Option E acknowledged as the interim privacy track if a private-data
deployment materializes before B decides. Option C stays parked
behind its two blockers. The decision that actually needs Victor is
B — it moves a frozen contract line — and it should be taken with
U-AG4's pass-level numbers on the table, not before.
