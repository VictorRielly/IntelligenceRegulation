## 0. The construction in one paragraph

Private attention is the FFN's `/2` discipline applied to the thirteen attention sections with three additions the inventory showed the FFN never needed. (1) **Every claim site becomes a reserved role with a pair or block mask** under FA3's rule, including the seventeen singleton A groups, the flipped-point RoPE claims, the two internal seam terminals and the six W groups under P3; each commitment gets a proximity pair; the ten histograms become committed A rows with their own pair/block masks; collapse is off. (2) **The six private claimed sums are never reconstructed by the verifier.** Each private-sum sumcheck sends its claimed sum once as a masked scalar `claim_s = s_s + c_s·G_s`, and the evaluation claim that used to define `s_s` publicly is proved inside a ν-batched host zero-check on the plane's own domain, exactly as the FFN's `gu_claim`/ZC20 and `d_claim`/ZC17 pairs do (FA6 §2.3): the host's claimed sum is `ν·claim_s + Γ_host` with `Γ_host = c_host·G_host − ν·c_s·G_s`. Where a public claimed sum today is a λ-combination of evaluations on **different** domains (the QKV slot), the slot splits so that each masked scalar is one plane evaluation on one domain. (3) **The nineteen LogUp helper sums collapse into one zero-sum row** `u` in H, `Σ_j u[j] = 0`, masked by a zero-sum pair plus a fresh pair (FA2 §14.5), with the table side folded in; only the value-0 sum claim survives, so the H residual is the public identity and nothing else. The resulting layout has been run through FA2's rank tool in repair form (§6): disclosed dimension 0 on every group and every commitment, H's residual the public identity only, and every negative control leaks. The CFS side keeps Opus's twelve-slot table with the two bilinear slots at two extension rounds, plus the split and host batching of §3, verified with FA6's criterion (§6). With A1–A4 supplied, the block simulator is FA8 §B applied to both components with the seams of FA3 §2.3; the fixed-challenge joint view of round 2 is the acceptance gate per channel once real receipts exist (§7).

## 1. Ground rules carried over unchanged (from round 1 §6 and FA3)

1. Every private plane is opened only through pair-masked claims `(1 − e)·T(p) + e·(R_re + u·R_im)(tail p)`; every proximity vector has a dedicated proximity pair; multi-row tables use block slots; no single base-field mask anywhere.
2. Every CFS slot has exactly the `(Σ, eval)` functional pair in one M per component, opened once with one θ; extension-round degree equals mask degree; zero-claimed slots publish `c·Σ`; private-sum slots publish `s + c·Σ`.
3. Every message is absorbed before the challenge that constrains it; no challenge before the fresh A root; header, public roots and endpoint policy first.
4. Refusals are functions of public inputs and challenges only, never resampled; one OS stream per component per attempt with an attempt nonce; owners consumed on success and failure; no seed reachable; nothing published on failure.
5. Histograms, helper sums and every terminal are committed or masked, never sent in the clear.
6. Acceptance gate per channel on captured real receipts: a zero-witness preimage with the same free dimension as the real witness (round 2's `zk60_joint_view.py` and `zk60_joint_cfs_view.py` are the tools), plus the block-level zero-witness acceptance test.

## 3. A2 — the CFS slot table, the private claimed sums, and the two bilinear slots

Opus's twelve-slot table (inventory §3.1) is adopted as the base: `(n_vars, degree)` with one extension round for every slot whose only public factor extends to zero, two for the two bilinear slots (score, PV) where `eq_g ≡ 1` at KV heads = 1 gives nothing to extend, and the P3 rule for the two weight matmuls. Every slot's `L_s + 256 ≤ 512` holds with the largest slot at `L = 62`. What this document adds is the treatment of the six private claimed sums (O-4, R6), which determines the batching and the final slot list.

### 3.1 The rule for a private claimed sum

Today the verifier forms the claimed sum of six sumchecks from earlier public claim values. Under any hiding profile those values are masked terminals, so the reconstruction is impossible (inventory §0 finding 2). The FFN's answer (FA6 §2.3) is adopted verbatim:

- the private-sum slot `s` publishes **one masked scalar** `claim_s = s_s + c_s·G_s`, absorbed before the slot's first round challenge; `G_s = Σ a_s` is the slot's mask total, enforced by the M functional opening;
- the plane evaluation that used to define `s_s` publicly becomes a term `ν·eq(p, x)·V(x)` inside a **host zero-check on the plane's domain**, where `V` is the public-coefficient combination of committed planes that reconstructs the value (for example `2^{sh}·q8 + rq`); the host's claimed sum is `ν·claim_s + Γ_host` with `Γ_host = c_host·G_host − ν·c_s·G_s` published, exactly ZC20's form;
- the host's terminal then evaluates `V` at the host's own ρ through pair-masked claims on the planes, which are sites the schedule of §2 already covers (the planes are opened at the host's ρ today);
- consequence: `s_s` never appears in the clear, the slot's own terminal (the inner product of pair-masked factors) closes against `claim_s`, and the mask accounting mixes only quantities already in the view's span (FA6 §2.3's argument, re-verified in §6 for each attention pair).

### 3.2 Splitting λ-combined private sums across domains

A masked scalar can carry one mask total, so a claimed sum that is a public λ-combination of evaluations on **different domains** cannot be hosted by one zero-check. The QKV slot's claimed sum `acc_q + λ_k·acc_k + λ_v·acc_v` combines a Q-domain value with two KV-domain values. It therefore **splits into three inner-product slots** (Q, K, V), each `(5, 2)` in P1 (`(6, 2)` in P3), each with its own masked scalar and its own host term: Q in the Q-domain zero-check, K and V in the KV-domain zero-check with two ν's. The three slots open `xn8` at three tails, which is three pair sites on the same rows (permitted, FA3 §2.3). A claimed sum that combines evaluations on **one** domain (the SS sums, the row sums, the score and PV and Wo sums) keeps its single slot and single masked scalar; the host term carries the whole combination as `Σ_i ν·coeff_i·eq(p_i, x)·plane_i(x)`.

## 4. A3 and the LogUp side — the helper sums, histograms and seams

### 4.1 One zero-sum row for the nineteen helper sums (O-1, R4)

Section 13 today checks `Σ_k hsum_k = Σ_tables Σ_rows m_i/(α − t_i)` in the clear from nineteen published sums and ten published histograms. In the private profile: the ten histograms are committed A rows (§2) and the table side is the same LogUp fraction relation the FFN proves for its tables (committed multiplicities, helper rows, zero-checks); the prover commits **one** additional H row `u` defined position-wise as the sum of the nineteen aligned helper columns minus the table-side helper columns, so that `Σ_j u[j] = 0` is exactly the LogUp identity; `u` is masked by a zero-sum pair (`Σ = 0` on both rows) and the sum claim's terminal by a fresh unconstrained pair (`h_u`/`h_up`, FA2 §14.5). The individual `hsum_k` are never published: each helper column is still opened as a pair-masked **evaluation** inside its domain's zero-check (the fraction relation), and the only sum claim on H is the value-0 claim on `u`. The rank check (§6) shows the surviving data functional on H is the public identity and nothing else; the negative control with nineteen published sums discloses nineteen dimensions.

### 4.2 Seams (A3) and the residual seam (A4)

- Internal FFN seam: `SeamXm8[0]`, `SeamZ16c[0]` are reserved pair roles on the attention A (+4 rows), claimed at the seam sumchecks' own challenge tails `tail(ρ_sx)`, `tail(ρ_sz)` drawn after both A roots and both M roots are absorbed, with S2's masked equality relation; the seam slots live on the FFN M (FA6 §6). The attention A is consumed once, after the seam claims are appended (R9–R11); a manifest names the seam roles and finalization refuses a consumed-role set different from the manifest (FA3 §4.1, §4.5).
- Residual seam between blocks (A4): `xres2(k)` vs `x_in(k+1)` as a masked equality between the two attention A's with its own CFS slot on block k+1's M and reserved pair rows on both A's; block k's A may not finalize before block k+1 binds it (two pending objects). This is A08's obligation; the first tiny P1 block (A07) has no residual seam, and its external endpoints use the `/2` IO mechanism (reserved pair rows on the attention A for `x_in` and `xres2`, two slots on the attention M, zero-claimed equality sumchecks against the caller's endpoint commitments).

## 5. Transcript order and refusals (R12, R16)

Per component: header and public roots and endpoint policy; the attention A root (all mask rows drawn before commit); the attention M root; then the first challenge; H root after the lookup challenges; the fourteen slots in A01's section order, each masked scalar or Γ absorbed before the slot's first round challenge, terminals and mask evaluations absorbed after the extension round; the seams; then one M opening and one opening per commitment in a fixed order. Refusals: `c_s = 0`, `e = 0`, degenerate pair coefficients, Fp-dependent proximity challenges, row-bit coordinates in Fp for block slots, a zero group-member coefficient, any count mismatch (R13), a non-empty collapse vector (R14). The recorded prover and verifier absorb sequences must equal a committed `transcript_order` list label by label; the round-2 transcript replay is the tool that checks it on real receipts once the labels exist.
