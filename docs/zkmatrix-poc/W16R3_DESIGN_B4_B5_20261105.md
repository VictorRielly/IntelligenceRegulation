Efficiency plan items B4 and B5 (`docs/zkmatrix-poc/ZK60_EFFICIENCY_PLAN_20261105.md`, §2, §3 Phase B, §4). Both
levers shrink the LogUp helper commitment `H`, which holds 2,514 of a W16R2 8B T128 block's 4,793 committed data rows

## 0. In brief

**Recommendation.**
1. **Build B4 into W16R3, as specified in §2.** It applies only to word checks, never to remainders.
   - **Conditions:**
     - Victor accepts one statement change, the *class gap* of §2.6;
     - the half-day microbenchmark of §5.3 confirms the savings side of the time model.
   - **Value:** robust but modest, about ×1.06–1.09 per block in both regimes.
   - **Risk:** low; it adds no new kind of zero-knowledge argument.
   - **Why now:** it pays for itself only inside the W16R3 version bump. Paid alone, a version bump would not be
     worth it.
2. **Do not freeze B5 into W16R3 unless two gates pass by the freeze**, otherwise ship it in a later profile:
   - a Metal GKR kernel reaches at least about 50% of the measured Goldilocks multiply rate (`kbench`);
   - the masked-GKR zero-knowledge construction passes its toy-field joint-view test and a review.
3. **Reasons for holding B5:**
   - On the Mac's GPU-bound stream B5 is ×0.86–1.07 alone and ×0.95–1.14 after B4 [MODEL].
   - Its gains show only where the host limits: one serial block, ×1.08–1.24; the RTX 5090 [MODEL / ESTIMATE].
   - Its other large gain is in receipt size (−31%), and B6 also attacks that.
4. **If both come, the order is B4 then B5.** B4 makes B5's tree 29% smaller.

**The biggest risks:**
- **B4, the class gap (§2.6).** A probabilistic check proves a wider class than the honest prover uses. A cheater can

- **B5:**

- First, the class ruling, which costs nothing.
- Then about half a day of guarded CPU/GPU timing with existing functions:

## 2. B4: probabilistic smallness, applied to the word checks only

### 2.1 The construction

The tier words are committed in `A` (`protocol.rs:1761`). After `M` (`:1771-1773`) and before β, γ, α (`:1779-1781`)
the schedule becomes:

1. **Squeeze a public bit matrix C** ∈ {0,1}^(k × E), with k = 128. It is either 1,146,880 bits per block, or one squeeze
   expanded by the transcript's XOF (§2.7).
2. **Form the combined values.** For tier B, `y_j[c'] = Σ_(r,t) C[j,(r,t)] · (A_r[16c' + t] + bias_r)`. Here r runs
   over the 304 tier-B rows (Y, Q, O, K, V, U16, S8, P) and t over 16 adjacent columns. So each combined value sums
   E = 4,864 entries, and there are 128 × 1,024 = 2^17 combined values.
   - Tier H is the same over H16's 128 rows with 32-column groups: E = 4,096 and 2^16 values.
   - The biases make every entry non-negative: 2^15 for the words, 127 for S8, 0 for P and 2^23 for H16.
3. **Commit `A2`**, a new small hiding commitment. It holds:
   - the combined values' upper limbs: tier B `t` (12 bits, 8 rows); tier H mid (16 bits) and top (4 bits), 8 rows;
   - the histograms for the families those limbs use: 16 (4 sub-planes), 12 (1) and 4 (1). These are 6 rows, needed
     because `A`'s histograms are fixed before C.
   - That is 22 data rows, plus 2·sites + 2 mask rows (D12a).
4. **β, γ, α, then `H`** as today. The combined limbs are ordinary W16 lookups on two new domains:
   - `Dom::Cb` (2^17 positions): `lo = y − 2^16·t` at width 16, and `t` at width 12. One helper.
   - `Dom::Ch` (2^16 positions): `lo` at width 16, `mid` at 16, `top` at 4. Two helpers.
   - The low limb is an expression, exactly like `word24` (`range16.rs:145-147`).
   - The TAB slot's table-helper relation reads `m_A + m_A2` for the six shared sub-planes.
5. **Two small zero-checks** (`Dom::Cb`, `Dom::Ch`) hold their helper relations (`argument.rs:1416-1433`). Each one's
   terminal claim on the virtual `Ỹ(ρ_j, ρ_c')` goes to a **combination sumcheck** over t:
   - The claim is `Σ_(r,t) W(r,t)·Ã_r(ρ_c', t)` with `W(r,t) = Σ_j eq(ρ_j, j)·C[j,(r,t)]`, which the verifier
     computes.
   - The sumcheck has 4 rounds for tier B and 5 for tier H, of degree 2.
   - It leaves `Σ_r W̃(r, ρ_t)·Ã_r(ρ_c', ρ_t)`. That is one `ZClaim` with 304 or 128 single-row parts at one column
     point. `pcs.rs:843-849` accepts it as **one** claim group, because the parts share the column weights.
   - A sumcheck over t is needed: without it, the 16 values of t would be 16 groups with different column weights,
     about 4.3 MB per block.
6. **Removed:**
   - the tier words' own lookups;
   - the AO and Z16 lookups (§2.3);
   - the `HT` plane (128 rows).
   - Per-position counts become L 2, D 16, Q 4, Kv 4, F 4 and S 4 [CODE, model `b4_counts_per_position`].

**Choice of parameters** [MODEL, `tiers`]:
- **Tier B**, L_B = 2^28: the honest maximum is 255,978,240, under 2^28 = 268,435,456.
- **Tier H**, L_H = 2^36: the honest maximum is 4,096·(2^24 − 1), under 2^36.
- **k = 128** makes the position counts powers of two at no extra cost.
- **Every family B4 uses already exists:** widths 16, 12 and 4 (`REVIEW_W16_20261101.md`, "logged families"). So T_d
  is unchanged.

### 2.2 What the check guarantees: the lemma

**Lemma (binary combination).**
- **Setting:**
  - v ∈ F_p^E is fixed before c;
  - one entry v_e has centred representative with |v_e| ≥ L;
  - c ∈ {0,1}^E is uniform.
- **Claim:** `Pr[Σ c·v mod p ∈ [0, L)] ≤ 1/2`.
- **Proof:**
  - Condition on every bit except c_e.
  - If both choices of c_e pass, then s and s + v_e are both in [0, L).
  - So v_e ≡ u with |u| < L, a contradiction.
- **Over k independent rows of C:** at most 2^-k. No union over entries or columns is needed, because one fixed bad
  entry already bounds the escape.

**Checked exhaustively** on F_61 with L = 6 (model `lemma`):
- **Binary:** the worst pass probability is exactly 1/2.
- **{−1, 0, 1}, FlashProve's choice:** the worst case is 2/3. The witness is v = (u + p)/2 with u small: s ± v are
  both small while s is not. So ternary needs k ≈ 171 against binary's 100 for 2^-100, at the same table width,
  because worst-case honest sums are equally wide. Binary dominates.
- **A public scale ≠ ±1 breaks the lemma.** With σ = 2, v = 1/2 mod p has σv = 1, small, while v itself is huge (the
  model's `scaled_coefficient_counterexample`).
  - **Rule for the implementation:** coefficients are exactly 0 or 1, and widths are never equalized by scaling.
  - That is why S8, P and H16 enter with their own biases, and H16 gets its own tier.

**What it guarantees after the check:** every tier-B entry has |v| < 2^28, so |w| < 2^28 + 2^15. Every H16 entry has
|h| < 2^36 + 2^23. That holds except with probability 2^-128 per attempt.

### 2.3 Which checks can move, and why remainders cannot

**Remainders stay exact.**
- A remainder proved only to (−L, L) with L ≫ 2^s leaves `q = floor(acc/2^s)` free by up to L/2^s.
- That would break D63(1)'s exact-output statement.
- This covers 1,144 of the 1,970 lookup rows.
- The isqrt digits stay exact too.

**Words can move.** Each word is pinned by an exact relation: a requant with an exact remainder, a residual or RoPE.
Its range only enforces its class and keeps no-wrap.

**Two words need no check at all.**
- `x1 − x − ao = 0` and `x2 − x1 − z = 0` (`argument_accuracy.rs:484-487`) hold with X, X1 and X2 exact.
- So ao ≡ x1 − x as field elements, a small integer with |ao| ≤ 2^16. The same holds for z.
- Their lookups are redundant except for the int16-against-int17 class, which the same ruling as §2.6 covers.

**P is not redundant.**
- With SR and SG exact, c2 and c3 (`:623-624`) pin P to the floor only if `P·Sum` does not wrap.
- An unbounded P solves c2 mod p with P = (mask·e·2^12 − sr)·Sum^-1 for a false sr.
- So P needs a bound. The tier-B bound 2^28 suffices (P·Sum < 2^45). Then P is the exact floor, so P has **no class
  gap**.

**The words kept exact, and why:**
- **X, X1:** the sums of squares wrap at the tier-B bound. The negative control in §2.4 shows it.
- **X2 and X:** the boundary planes, and the next-token stage reads the last block's X2.
- **YM:** D12 item 4 (`ZK60_DECISIONS.md:37, :423-427`).
- **QR, KR:** the bilinear score.
- **Keeping YM, QR and KR exact costs no `H` row.** D 16, Q 4 and Kv 4 lookups give the same ⌈lookups/2⌉ helpers
  either way.

### 2.5 Completeness: perfect, by construction

**Why perfect.** L is the **worst-case** honest sum, Σ_r G·B_r, not a concentration bound. So an honest witness whose
words are in today's classes always passes, for every C [CODE / MODEL: `honest_fits_L`]. That holds because the
prover keeps today's rule:
- `refuse_lookup_misses` refuses before `A`'s masks and before any challenge (`protocol.rs:465-475, :630-639`);
- under B4 it must also check the tier words' **narrow** classes in the sealing pass, because they are no longer looked
  up one by one.

**What that means:**
- There is no retry, and no refusal that depends on C.
- FlashProve's open "retry repair" (`architecture.md:51` in Astra's comparison, `results/zk60_astra_20261026/`; the
  comparison folder is in the main checkout and not committed) does not arise.
- It arises there because a tighter-than-worst-case bound makes honest failures possible.
- **The price** is a wider top limb: 12 bits against about 8 under a concentration bound. The biased binary sum has
  mean near 2^26 and standard deviation near 2^20 [MODEL: √4864·2^15/2]; a window of ±7.5σ is about 2^24. That costs
  nothing here, since 2 limbs are needed either way.

### 2.6 The class gap: the ruling B4 needs

**The gap.** The honest prover proves runs whose words are in today's classes: int16, int8 S8, the 24-bit H16. The
check proves only |w| < 2^28 (2^36 for H16). A cheater can therefore make an accepted receipt for an input whose
exact-integer run has some tier word in the *band* between the two. The output it attests is `forward_ext(x)`, the
exact integer computation, which agrees with the model wherever today's classes hold.

**What it breaks.** `CircuitIff` is one iff with one `admissible` (`Statements.lean:87-99, :146-148`), and T1a
quantifies over every admissible input (`:150-160`).
- Making `admissible` the wide class breaks T1a: honest proofs of band inputs would fail with a probability that
  depends on C, which D66 forbids.
- Keeping it narrow breaks the ⇒ direction.

**The ruling, two options for Victor:**
- **(a) A two-class `CircuitIff`** for probabilistic-smallness profiles:
  - satisfiable ⇒ admissible_wide ∧ y = forward_ext;
  - admissible_narrow ⇒ satisfiable;
  - forward_ext = forward on the narrow class;
  - the honest prover refuses outside the narrow class, before any commitment. That is the declared "whether it is
    served" leakage, with one constant refusal (D65(2)).
  - This changes an approved statement (D63), so it needs Victor's approval.
  - It is also the shape of FlashProve's own claim: values "within a looser bound", plus no-overflow conditions
    (`upstream/docs/protocol.md:159`).
- **(b) No B4.** Without the ruling, only P (no gap) could move. P alone does not pay for a combination slot.

**Not affected:**
- P;
- every remainder;
- the boundary planes;
- YM, so D12 item 4 holds as written.

### 2.8 Refusals (D66: challenge-only)

**No new refusal.**
- **Before any challenge:** a word outside its narrow class refuses before `A` (§2.5). That refusal is a function of
  the input only, as today, and the text is D65(2)'s single string.
- **After C:** an honest combined value is always in range (§2.5), so nothing refuses.
- **A zero LogUp denominator for a combined limb:** today's table-side rule refuses whenever α hits **any** entry of
  a used family (`range16.rs:244-264`; `protocol.rs:981-992`). Honest limbs are table values, so the rule depends on
  (α, γ) and public data alone: class (C), exactly as `dd_challenge_refusal` made the dictionary
  (`protocol.rs:1062-1080`). B4 adds no family.

### 2.9 Hiding (masks for the combined values)

**Nothing new is sent in the clear except C**, which is public.
- **The combined values** are linear in the hidden activations.
  - They are never sent. Their limbs are in `A2`, a hiding Ligero commitment in ZK mode with q_pad = Q = 280 per row
    (`pcs.rs:1-38`).
  - The low limb is an expression in `A` and `A2`.
- **The two new zero-checks**:
  - claimed sum: 0;
  - round messages: CFS masks, committed in `M` with the other slots' masks (witness-free, so `M` stays before C);
  - terminal claims on `A2` and `H`: each through a reserved mask pair.
- **The combination sumcheck** is masked the same way.
  - Its claimed sum `Ỹ(ρ)` is private and is sent masked (S + c·G).
  - Its final claim on `A`, at a new column point, takes a new `A` site: +2 `A` mask rows per site, 4 for the two
    tiers.
- **The histograms in `A2`** (16-bit families of subset sums) are hidden like MU8 today. The privacy re-check must
  list them, as the W16 review asked for the 16-bit histograms.

**For the proofs, the joint view gains:**
- one hiding commitment;
- three masked sumchecks of existing shape;
- masked evaluations of private planes at fresh points.

These are instances of the existing families `HidingSumcheck`, `HidingSumcheckJoint`, `HidingRows`, `HidingOpening`,
`HidingOpeningSum` and `HidingMerkle`. No new kind of argument is needed.

## 3. B5: LogUp-GKR for what remains

### 3.1 What it adds

The design is H111, from H107–H116 (`research/zkp-efficiency/experiments/logup-gkr-20261101/README.md`,
`ZK_DESIGN_NOTE.md`; handoff `20261101-logup-gkr-w16.md`).

**What it replaces.** Every committed helper, the TAB/ZS helper work and, in the all-lookups variant, the exp/ACT
helpers too. With them goes the whole `H` commitment and its opening (`protocol.rs:1991`).

**The fractional-sumcheck forest.**
- Leaves are queries `(1, α − γ·w − v)` and table entries `(−m, α − γ·w − t)`. A pad repeats an entry with m = 0.
- **B5 alone:** 35,025,844 leaves (range, exp/ACT queries and tables) [MODEL, `gkr_leaves`]. They form a forest of
  2^25 + 2^21.
- **After B4:** 24,736,692 leaves, a forest of 2^24 + 2^23.
- So B4 cuts the tree by 29%. Without a forest or a ragged bottom layer, 24.7 M leaves would pad to 2^25 and the cut
  would vanish.
- **Layers:** 25 layer sumchecks with the smaller tree batched in. Layer k has k − 1 rounds of degree 3, a last round of
  degree 7 (the mask), a child-bit challenge r_k and a batching λ_k. That is about 300–325 rounds. The prototype's
  single 2^25 tree had 300, 66 of them GPU-scale.
- **Leaf claim:** one linear term per domain zero-check, at the GKR's leaf point restricted to that domain
  (ZK_DESIGN_NOTE §3).
  - So there are no new opening points, and D12's one opening per commitment holds.
  - The `Cb`/`Ch` leaf claims go through §2.1's combination sumcheck.

### 3.2 Masking

From the root-blinding handoff (`handoffs/20261030-streamed-logup-root-blinding.md`) and the ZK note §1–§2:
- **Root.** Two blinding leaves, `(s·r1, r1)` and `(−s·r2, r2)`, are committed in `A` before α.
  - With a forest, the natural placement is one leaf per tree. Each tree's root fraction is then the uniformly masked
    partial sum S_i ± s.
  - Their pairing must be **constrained** (p_L − s·q_L = 0, p_R + s·q_R = 0 in a zero-check). Otherwise mask leaves
    can fix a false identity, as the 1030 handoff warns.
  - Two realizations are known negatives:
    - a shared scale leaks the quadratic character of D (H109);
    - base-field, disclosed or reused scales, and `(0,0)` leaves.
- **Layers k ≥ 2.** Libra masks `p' = p + Z_k·R` with Z_k over the **parent** coordinates only. Over every coordinate,
  Z vanishes at the Boolean child bit; tessera PR #325 hit the same leak.
  - So only each layer's last round sees Z ≠ 0, which raises its degree to 7.
  - CFS masks (degree ≥ 7) go on every layer's rounds.
  - The leaf layer is masked too.
- **Budget:** `M` +48 rows packed or +144 unpacked (today 50). This is witness-free, so it can be committed off the
  critical path.

### 3.3 Soundness

- **α:** the rational identity has the same degree as today, so (N + 2T_d − 1)/p² = 2^-102.93 (B5 alone) or
  2^-103.43 (after B4).
- **GKR rounds:** at most 7/p² = 2^-125.2 each, about 1,125/p² = 2^-117.9 in sum. The sum stays inside F44's
  numerator premise: about 3,400 + 1,125 against 2^16.
- **The `H` opening leaves the zone.**
- **Rate and zone:** the rate is unchanged (2^-102.93 alone, 2^-103.43 after B4). The zone is 2^-102.74 alone and
  2^-103.16 after B4 (model `soundness`).
- **The blinding pair** is fixed before α and constrained to cancel, so it cannot offset a false identity.

### 3.4 Refusals

**The verifier** checks `q_root ≠ 0` (ZK note §1).

**The prover's rule must be stated as the public one,** to stay challenge-only (D66): refuse iff α hits an admissible
table entry.
- Honest queries are table values, so this coincides with q_root = 0.
- The blinding r's are the prover's own nonzero draws, resampled rather than refused.

## 5. Recommendation, risks and the first experiments

### 5.1 Is it worth building, and in what order?

1. **Ask Victor for the class ruling of §2.6** (option a or b). Without (a), B4 does not apply.
2. **Run the half-day microbenchmark (§5.3).** If at least 60% of the modelled saving appears, freeze B4 into the W16R3
   design with B1–B3. Then build it: about 2–3 weeks [ESTIMATE].
   - **Shape and layout:** `Dom::Cb` and `Dom::Ch`, `A2` with its row derivation, the slot schedule, and a new profile
     string (D17).
   - **Witness:** the GEMM, the limbs, the histograms, and the narrow-class pre-commit check.
   - **Argument:** two small zero-checks, the combination sumcheck, TAB reading `A2`, the lookup lists without the
     moved words, no `HT`.
   - **Protocol:** C after `M`; commit and absorb `A2` before β; open `A2`.
   - **Metal:** the IR for the changed slots.
   - **Malicious controls:**
     - a tier word at L, refused;
     - a **band word, accepted by design**, which documents the gap;
     - a wrong top limb;
     - a non-unit coefficient;
     - histograms moved between `A` and `A2`.
3. **B5 in parallel:** the Metal GKR kernel experiment (§5.3) and the toy-field joint-view test of the root-blinding
   handoff. Decide B5's inclusion at the W16R3 freeze, and include it only if:
   - the kernel reaches at least 50% of kbench;
   - the ZK construction has a reviewed simulator sketch.

   Otherwise B5 goes to a later profile with B6, where the receipt question is settled.

### 5.2 The open risks

**B4:**
1. **The class gap (§2.6):** a change to an approved statement.
2. **About 1-bit no-wrap margins** (down requant, PV naive). They need shape-level asserts.
3. **The small fixed costs** (`A2` commit, two slots, extra claim groups) could eat a third to a half of the saving. The
   haircut covers about a quarter.
4. **The coefficient trap:** a non-unit or signed coefficient silently weakens the lemma.
5. **The DD profiles** are not covered. W16R3's DD variant needs the same pass.
6. **The tier words lose their per-value lookup.** Any later relation that assumes int16 for Y, Q, O, K, V, U16 or S8
   must be checked against 2^28.

**B5:**
1. **The joint-view simulation is unproven,** including the forest's root masks and the per-domain leaf-claim shares.
2. **Its sign on the Mac stream** depends on an unmeasured GPU efficiency (break-even 47–48%).
3. **The `M` rows' commit** on the critical path if it is not precomputed.
4. **A slot reorder,** which needs D17's new profile string.
5. **A forest or ragged bottom layer is required.** Otherwise the 35.0 M leaves pad to 2^26 (B5 alone), and B4's
   24.7 M pad to 2^25.

### 5.3 What would falsify the model, and the cheapest experiments

**B4's savings side.** The model assumes:
- `H` commit time is linear in rows down to 1,788;
- slot 18 scales with tables (19 → 16);
- a 30-row `A2` commit costs ≤ 8 ms.

**The cheapest first experiment** (about half a day): an ignored test in the gpu-metal tree, run under the resource
guard. Its timings:
- `pcs::commit_zk_owned` at the W16R2 geometry (rate 1/2, Q 280, 2^14 columns) on 30, 1,788 and 2,602 random rows;
- a copy of slot 18's zero-check at 2^21 with 16 and 19 tables;
- slot 16's builder at 2^17 with 6 tables, as the `Cb` proxy.

It needs no protocol change and no new profile.

**Kill rule:** B4 is dropped if the measured savings are under 60% of the model's 65–70 ms, so that the net gain is
under ×1.04.

**B5:** the Metal kernel for the leaf and virtual layers, fan-in 4 (H111's next step), at 2^24 leaves, under the guard.
It takes 2–3 days.
- **Kill rule:** it achieves under 48% of the kbench base-multiply rate. B5 would then slow the Mac stream after B4.

**Already checked here, at no cost** (model.py):
- the lemma, exhaustively on F_61: binary 1/2, ternary 2/3, scaled counterexample;
- every relation's no-wrap at the B4 bounds, with the negative control;
- the row and lookup counts, against the census.
