## 1. What survived the attack

- **The load-bearing idea.** In this design the third party holds the hidden
  *values* and never any commitment randomness; every polynomial commitment is
  generated by the prover inside the proof. That is exactly the configuration
  D8 and D11 do *not* bind. The draft never claims it closes them, and it is
  right not to.
- **Nothing is opened twice and no commitment root crosses a proof boundary**,
  including when proofs are chained through handles. D12 is respected in the
  letter. The accepted paths stay frozen and the gadget is a new profile (D17).
- **Every arithmetic cell of the draft reproduced exactly.** Its errors are in
  the model underneath the arithmetic, which is the more dangerous kind.
- **The permutation parameters the costs rest on are confirmed** from public
  sources by the check, four ways: width 12, 8 full and 22 partial rounds, the
  seventh-power S-box, 118 S-box applications per permutation; and
  `gcd(7, p − 1) = 1` by arithmetic. Capacity 4 gives the claimed collision
  level, with the usual caveat that the bound assumes an ideal permutation.
- **The packing citations are real**: both endpoint planes are already
  byte-decomposed and range-proved in the toy block.

## 2. Rulings

**S-1 (B-4, B-7): the draft's headline finding is withdrawn, and the register
is not changed by it.** The prover indeed has no "next index" operation
(`Coord` is `Rho` / `Flip` / `Fixed`), but that is *not implemented*, not
inexpressible, and it is not needed: unrolling `k` absorptions inside one leaf
uses only `Coord::Fixed` and costs a factor `1 + 1/k`, with `k` free. So a
binary tree is not forced and **D18's count is not "two times low"**. SO-7 is
refused. What the check did establish about D18's figures, and what I am
recording in the register: the public figure (+5.5%) follows from its stated
basis; **the private figure (+12.6%) has no published derivation and could not
be reproduced**; both stay labelled counted and untested.

**S-2 (B-2): the handle encodes what it hashes.** Element count, tree shape,
leaf-versus-internal separation and a padding rule for a ragged last chunk
all enter the hash, on the model of the slow line's handle, which already
absorbs its element count (`zkvm-block/statement/src/lib.rs:65-78`). Without
them collision resistance fails as specified, and the binding half of the
statement means nothing.

**S-3 (B-1): the assumption is named honestly, and the revision must scope a
design that does not need it.** "Random-oracle hiding" is not an honest label
for an algebraic permutation used with a secret salt: every published analysis
of the candidates is of an unkeyed public hash, and algebraic attacks are
strongest in exactly the setting a salted handle of a low-entropy plane
creates. **My direction for the revision, to be attacked by the next check
like any other claim: a two-layer handle.** The algebraic hash is computed
over the packed plane *without any secret* and its root is never published;
the public handle is a **standard** hash (the slow line's SHA-256 or the
transcript's BLAKE3) of `tag ‖ context ‖ role ‖ element count ‖ shape ‖ salt ‖
algebraic root`, proved inside the proof by one or two byte-hash compressions
— a cost that does not grow with the plane. Then hiding rests on a standard
hash with a 256-bit salt, which is the assumption the project owner already
accepted for the slow line in D7; and only collision resistance, the mode the
literature actually analyses, rests on the algebraic permutation. The inner
digests are hidden intermediates on the prover's own commitment like any
other. This also removes the salt-position problem the check found (a salt in
one leaf leaves every other leaf digest an unsalted hash of a 32-byte chunk).
**Unchecked, mine, and priced by nobody yet.**

**S-4 (B-3): the sealer's threat model is stated, and it is smaller than the
check feared.** Whoever seals a plane holds its plaintext, so no mechanism
protects that plane *from the sealer*; and a malicious *prover* can always
publish its own witness, so zero knowledge never protected a data subject
from the prover. A salt ground to make the handle carry information is a
channel between parties who could talk anyway. What the memo must say: who the
sealer is in each deployment (a regulator sealing its own test input; a user's
client sealing the user's own prompt; an upstream prover sealing its own
output), that the salt is 256 fresh bits from the operating system per handle,
never reused and never derived from the plane, and that **an unverifiable salt
is worth exactly the sealer's diligence** — acceptable because the sealer is
entitled to the plaintext. Attested salt generation is recorded as a possible
hardening, not a requirement. This item stops being blocking once that text
exists.

**S-5 (B-5): the GPU zero-check is capped at degree 4** (`#define SC_DEG 4`,
`hashline/src/gpu/kernels.cu:601`), and the private block has no device path
at all. The default pricing is therefore the split form, two committed values
per S-box. Any GPU-denominated figure is conditional on a kernel that does not
exist and must say so in the same sentence.

**S-6 (B-6): one wiring is priced consistently.** The draft priced 128 claim
sites while its own text says the level-wise form multiplies them by the
depth: 2,124 at Mistral width, 1,770 at the 125M shape, 708 at the toy, where
the gadget would then exceed the receipt it rides on. The claim-site count is
the first thing to measure, and the check's proposal is accepted: **the first
step, when option B is scheduled, is a four-leaf tree in the layout builder
alone, which prints its own site and row counts in hours and proves nothing.**

**S-7 (B-8, the choice of hash): the security owner recommends no hash
today.** The check found Rescue-Prime Optimized cheaper than the draft's
choice (12.0 committed values per absorbed element against 29.5; 24.0 in the
split form), and it reports two developments from public sources that I have
not verified myself: a February 2026 paper (eprint 2026/306) improving
collision attacks on *a* recommended 128-bit Poseidon2 parameter set, target
set unconfirmed, and the largest funder of that primitive's cryptanalysis
dropping it from its roadmap in August 2026 on performance grounds. No
published attack reaches the round numbers in question. **The choice is a
project-owner security acceptance of the same kind as D3, D4 and D7, and it
is taken when option B is scheduled, with the literature read again that
week.** Under S-3's two-layer design the question shrinks to collision
resistance only, and an all-standard-hash handle (no algebraic hash at all)
must be priced beside it as the conservative alternative; the check could not
confirm any byte-hash cost figure either way.

**S-8 (chaining through handles).** Compatible with D12's letter; when it is
built, D12 needs an amendment that names handle chaining as its own form and
says that a published handle of an intermediate plane is **prior history for
every later proof and analysis**. Under P1 a public handle is permitted
(`ZK60_PRIVACY_REQUIREMENTS.md:18-19`). What the project owner must be told in
plain words before accepting it: linkage between proofs that share a plane is
public, **retroactive and permanent**, and a party who guesses a low-entropy
plane and knows the salt can confirm the guess.
