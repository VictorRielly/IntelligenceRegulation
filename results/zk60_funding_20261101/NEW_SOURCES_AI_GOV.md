## 1. Plain summary

- **Fastest good fit: the GTR AI Safety Fund (Berlin).** It is rolling and
  names "international agreements and verification mechanisms" in scope.
  It needs a 1–3 page proposal plus a CV, gives first feedback within 21
  days, and takes individuals with no entity. Money could arrive in Dec
  2026 or Jan 2027.
- **Second fast option: BlueDot Seed Grants.** They pay $20k–$200k for 6–12
  months, are paid to individual founders, and decide in about 4 weeks. They
  assume full-time work, so pitch the grant as paying collaborators.
- **Most urgent: BlueDot Incubator Week**, 16–20 Nov in San Francisco. The
  application deadline is 11 Nov (secondary source), with up to $200k in
  grants "within two weeks". It wants founders "ready to leave whatever
  you're doing now", which clashes with the new day job.
- **Best fit, but slower: ARIA Scaling Trust (UK).** It pays £100k–£3m,
  accepts individuals and non-UK applicants, and Track 3.3 names "Succinct
  and inspectable proofs for correct execution and delegation". The 31 Oct
  cut-off has just passed and the next date is not yet published. Money is
  around April 2027 at the earliest. All software must be MIT + Apache-2.0
  and the work must benefit the UK.
- **Equity with fast forms:** SAIF ($100k; short web form) and Juniper
  (email). Halcyon (email) offers grants and venture money. All need the
  Delaware C-corp first.
- **Mostly closed or ineligible:**
  - Closed: UK AISI Challenge Fund (also academic and non-profit only),
    Cooperative AI Foundation, Middle Powers RFP, the Coefficient
    capability-evaluations RFP, CAIS compute, FLI and Longview.
  - No 2027 dates yet: SFF.
  - US government options here are slow or need a university lead.

## 3. Details: High and Med fits, plus the urgent Incubator Week

### 3.1 GTR AI Safety Fund (Global Technology Risk Foundation, Berlin): High

- **URL / status:** https://www.gtr-foundation.org/gtr-ai-safety-fund.
  Open, rolling. "Occasional deadlines may be set for particular funding
  windows." (Verified)
- **Next deadline:** none; rolling. (Verified)
- **Amount:** not stated. Support runs "from a single grant to multi-year
  support". The fund is about €50M over 3–7 years (secondary:
  aisafety.com, Partial). Fundable costs named in the FAQ: initial funding
  for a promising organisation, research, **compute**, fellowships and
  travel. In-kind help includes workspace and **hiring support**. (Verified)
- **Eligibility:**
  - (a) Individuals: yes, at any career stage. No geographic focus.
  - (b) Part-time lead: not addressed. "You don't need to know yet what
    exactly you want to do."
  - (c) For-profits: "may be considered", but "the individual funding
    structure ... may differ".
  - (d) No partner needed. (Verified)
  - Payment to a US individual from a German gGmbH is not addressed
    (UNVERIFIED).
- **Scope:** "Anything that credibly reduces catastrophic risks from
  advanced AI". The named areas include "cybersecurity" and "international
  agreements and verification mechanisms". (Verified)
- **Open-source / publicity:** none stated. (Verified as absent)
- **Time to decision:** "initial feedback, either a decision or an
  invitation to a call, within 21 days". The time from decision to money
  is not stated. (Verified)
- **Form (verbatim):**
  - Proposal "ideally 1 page and no more than 3 pages", plus a **CV**.
    Uploads are capped at **10 MB**.
  - The proposal must answer three questions:
    1. "Describe your work or the problems you are interested in working on."
    2. "Tell us about your background, what you have built or done so far,
       and why ai safety matters to you."
    3. "Tell us why GTR is the right funder for this work. What does it need
       that other sources can't easily provide, and where else are you
       seeking or have you sought funding?"
  - Submission: the Apply section on the same page
    (`#ai-apply`). Nominations go to grants@gtr-foundation.org. (Verified)
- **Fit: High.** It is the only open, fast funder whose stated scope names
  verification mechanisms. Pitch: cryptographic verification of which
  model ran and what it output, without disclosing the data, as a building
  block for AI agreements and audits. Ask for 2 collaborators plus GPU time.

### 3.2 BlueDot Seed Grants: Med

- **URL / status:** https://bluedot.org/grants/seed (index:
  https://bluedot.org/grants). Open; no deadline shown. (Verified)
- **Amount:** "$20k-$200k for 6-12 months". The listed uses are living
  costs, compute and running costs, and travel. (Verified)
- **Eligibility:**
  - (a) Individuals: yes. "Right now we pay Seed Grants to individual
    founders". Payment to an organisation is possible if you ask.
  - (b) Part-time lead: risky. The page funds "Living costs, so you can work
    on the project full-time". Paying collaborators is not named as a use.
  - (c) For-profit: "In some cases, where the work clearly serves the public
    good ... These grants take longer to approve."
  - (d) No partner needed. A BlueDot course is not required ("Anyone can
    apply").
  - Some countries cannot receive funds (China, India and Russia are
    named). Check this against Victor's banking and citizenship.
    (Verified)
- **Open-source / publicity:** none stated. Reporting: "A few lines" each
  month and a final report. Unspent funds go back if the project stops.
  (Verified)
- **Time to decision:** "about 4 weeks"; payment timing after signing is
  not stated. (Verified)
- **Form:**
  - The form "takes about 45 minutes".
  - When asked what you are seeking funding for, choose "Starting an
    organization".
  - The criteria headings are: problem selection, a well-reasoned
    hypothesis, evidence you can make it happen, and a concrete plan.
  - The individual form fields and the form URL were not visible to the
    fetcher; the form sits behind the bluedot.org login. (Partial)
- **Fit: Med.** It is fast and open to an individual. The AI-safety framing

### 3.6 Mercor AI Safety Fund: Low-Med

- **URL / status:** the Mercor careers page (script-rendered; read through
  the General Catalyst mirror). Apply at
  https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/mercor/1d59ce50-4207-4d95-b7fd-5a01e90b0897.
  Posted 16 Sep 2026; no deadline listed. (Partial)
- **Amount:** a $5M fund; "Award size and mix depend on the project". It
  covers researcher hours, API costs, event stipends and expert time.
  (Partial)
- **Eligibility:**
  - Applicants must be US- or UK-based (residence, not citizenship).
  - Eligible: universities, non-profits, public benefit corporations and
    "small startups that do not sell AI training data".
  - Awards are paid to the researcher's institution, so (c) a new company
    or (d) a partner is needed.
  - Individuals can apply with "a specific, well-scoped question".
    (Partial)
- **Topics:** misalignment, sandbox escape, evaluation awareness,
  interpretability, oversight and control, and red-teaming. "Verification"
  appears only in the intro. (Partial)
- **Terms:**
  - Grantees keep ownership, but Mercor gets a **non-exclusive licence
    including commercial use**.
  - Grantees are expected to publish under an open licence that permits
    commercial use.
  - For the award plus **12 months**, no work with named Mercor competitors
    on similar benchmarks or evaluations.
  - Mercor gets early access and the held-out test-set rights on benchmark
    projects. (Partial)
- **Form:** an Expression of Interest, a "one- or two-page document" with
  three parts: team, background and research accomplishments; the
  proposed project; and outputs and impact, with "directionally correct
  timelines and resource requirements". No confidential information.
  (Partial)
- **Fit: Low-Med.** Its scope is model evaluation, not verification
  infrastructure. Pitch only as "evaluations whose results can be checked
  without revealing data or model". The licence terms bind outputs.

### 3.7 ARIA Scaling Trust (Tracks 2 and 3): High, but slow

- **URL / status:**
  - Programme: https://aria.org.uk/opportunity-spaces/trust-everything-everywhere/scaling-trust
  - Funding page: https://aria.org.uk/opportunity-spaces/trust-everything-everywhere/scaling-trust/funding
  - Portal: https://aria.grantplatform.com/
  - "Rolling programme funding is currently open for Scaling Trust (Track
    2 + 3)." Programme total £49.8m. (Verified)
- **Deadlines:**
  - Last cut-off: 31 Oct 2026, 14:00 GMT, per Innovate UK Business Connect
    (Partial); the ARIA page gives the date only.
  - Review runs 1–30 Nov, with notification on 30 Nov.
  - Next cut-off: "Dates for the next cycle will be confirmed soon." The
    cycle "repeats every three months" (IUK listing), so late January
    2027 is likely, but that date is **UNVERIFIED**.
  - Anything submitted now rolls into the next cycle. ARIA "may choose not
    to make awards in a given review cycle". (Verified)
- **Amount:** £100k–£3m per team, 3–18 months, renewal possible.
  - Track 2 (tooling): £200k–£2m, 4–6 teams.
  - Track 3 (research): 3 research centres of about £2–3m, plus 4–12
    smaller teams.
  - ARIA funds 100% of costs. (Verified, call document v1.0)
- **Scope that fits:**
  - Track 3.3 "Foundations of Generative Security" lists "Succinct and
    inspectable proofs for correct execution and delegation".
  - Track 2.2 "Components" includes "Report – input execution trace →
    outputs succinct convincing statement" and "libraries for
    cryptography".
  - Pitch: a hiding-commitment proof of an agent's LLM step, as the
    "report" component for agent-to-agent trust. (Verified)
- **Eligibility:**
  - (a) Individuals: yes ("including individuals"), and "Applicants can be
    based in the UK or abroad".
  - (c) Companies of all sizes are eligible.
  - (d) No partner is required.
  - (b) Part-time lead: weak. ARIA "usually prefer[s] any lead or key
    researchers to be spending at least 50%, ideally 80%, of their time".
    Collaborators would need to be the key researchers.
  - **UK benefit:** the strongest cases spend more than 50% of costs in
    the UK. Others must show plans that "boost the net impact of a
    programme in the UK". Events are UK-based, so budget for travel.
  - Applicants pass identity and national-security checks. (Verified)
- **Open-source / IP:** "all software produced under Track 2 (Tooling) and
  Track 3 (Fundamental Research) must be released under a permissive
  open-source licence. We require a dual-licence approach under MIT and
  Apache 2.0." Background IP stays with the applicant. (Verified)
- **Time to decision:** about 1 month of review after a cut-off. There is a
  1-hour call within 10 working days of selection, and the contract is
  signed within 6 weeks. ARIA does not negotiate its terms and gives no
  feedback to rejected applicants. (Verified)
- **Form (Proposal Guidelines, verbatim where quoted):**
  - **Format:** "max 10 pages, (including diagrams, excluding
    references)"; "single line spacing"; "Font: Arial. Colour: black.
    Size: 11-point font or larger"; "At least 0.5” margins"; "PDF only".
    A 1-pager is acceptable for short projects.
  - **Section 0:** "Summary of your proposal in 500 words max".
  - **Section 1, Programme & Technical:** the track (2.1, 2.2, 3.1–3.4;
    one proposal per track); the detailed idea with visual aids and data;
    "A comprehensive list of the known technical risks/unknowns";
    differentiation; activities, metrics, milestones, dependencies; and a
    Project Plan.
  - **Section 2, The Team:** who does the work and their time share;
    whether they are in place; short bios ("we discourage you from
    submitting CVs"); third parties; coordination; competency gaps; and
    motivation.
  - **Section 3, Administrative**, answered in the portal outside the
    10-page cap:
    - a cost breakdown on ARIA's spreadsheet;
    - any co-funding;
    - background IP;
    - other funding sought;
    - freedom to operate, conflicts of interest and export restrictions;
    - an uploaded **commercial hypothesis**;
    - whether the majority of the work is outside the UK, with UK plans and
      a timeline;
    - organisational authorisation;
    - confirmation that the funding terms were read.
  - **Evaluation criteria:** Worth Shooting For; Differentiated; Well
    defined; Responsible; Intrinsic motivation; Benefit to the UK.
  - The rolling call may use an updated document; the one read is v1.0,
    10 Feb 2026. (Verified)
- **Fit: High** on topic, open source and size. Two things count against
  it: the time commitment and the UK-benefit test. A UK-based collaborator
  doing most of the work would strengthen it.
