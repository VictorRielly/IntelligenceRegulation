# Philanthropic and ecosystem grant options (researched 2026-10-28)

Scope: AI-safety / AI-governance philanthropy, ZK / crypto ecosystem grants,
child-safety and content-provenance funds. Government grants and investors are
covered by other agents.

Status: COMPLETE (research only; nothing was applied for, registered or sent).
"Verified" = read on the program's own page on 2026-10-28. "Partial" = the
official site blocked the fetcher (HTTP 403), so the text comes from
search-engine excerpts of that official page. "UNVERIFIED" = no official

## 1. Plain summary

- **Most of the AI-safety and ZK money is shut right now.** EF ESP has no
  Wishlist or RFP item open. Coefficient Giving's AI-governance and
  technical-safety RFPs are closed. SFF, Longview, Schmidt, the FMF AI Safety
  Fund and the UK Alignment Project have no open round. The Long-Term Future
  Fund has been wound down.
- **Fastest realistic money:** a public Manifund project. Regrantors and
  AISTOF fund "in days, not months", typically $10k-$400k, but only if one of
  them picks it up. Next is **Lightcone Commons**: rolling, open to
  individuals and for-profits, a ~$45M first round and monthly rounds. Apply
  by about **Nov 24** for the round closing mid-December; the answer comes
  about 2 weeks after close and the money about a month later (roughly late
  January 2027).
- **Deadline this week:** the **Foresight AI Nodes** RFP closes **Fri 31 Oct
  2026, 23:59 PDT**. It pays $30k-$100k+, takes individuals, requires the
  funded work to be open-sourced, and decides about 3 months after the
  deadline.
- **Best-fit large grant:** Coefficient Giving's **Project Tailwind**, open
  to founders. It gives pre-seed research grants of $200k-$2M and seed
  funding of $2M-$20M, decided after an invited pitch. Pitch it as an
  organization for privacy-preserving verification of AI governance claims.
  Its off-topic filter rejects pure ZK or child-safety pitches.
- **Child-safety and provenance funds have no open call.** Safe Online's
  2026 call was narrow and is done, and C2PA/CAI give no grants. A later
  Safe Online open call would need a legal entity.
- **With no entity yet:** Lightcone, the EA Funds TAI Fund, Manifund,
  Foresight and Kusama all take individuals. SFF needs a fiscal sponsor or
  an incorporated company with a bank account. Safe Online needs a
  registered entity.

## 3. Top 6 ranked (fit x speed x amount)

**1. Lightcone Commons: the best mix of size, speed and eligibility.**
*Next step:* write the 4-section application (what, what the money buys,
who, logistics) and submit by about **Nov 24** to make the round that closes
mid-December. The application is a live document that can be edited after
submission.
*Needs first:* nothing formal. An individual gets fiscal sponsorship if the
work counts as charitable; a company can apply directly. A public technical
note or repo helps evaluators.
*Angle:* **AI-governance verification.** Third parties (auditors, regulators,
platforms) can check *which model produced an output* and *what a safety
classifier said* without seeing user data. Give the measured 8B numbers,
labelled honestly: internal privacy acceptance, no external audit.

**2. Foresight AI Nodes, call II: the deadline is in 3 days.**
*Next step:* submit by **31 Oct 2026, 23:59 PDT**; ask for $50k-$100k for a
bounded milestone, such as an externally reviewable verifier plus benchmark
for private-inference proofs.
*Needs first:* agreeing that the **funded work product is open-sourced**.
Scope it to a separable component. Being willing to work in person at the
SF node helps ("strongly prioritize").
*Angle:* "scalable verification and proof techniques" for coordination and
accountability; AI insurance and open governance.

**3. A Manifund public project (plus asking AISTOF): the fastest if it
lands.**
*Next step:* post a project page with a funding goal and a minimum, then
ask AISTOF and a few regrantors on the platform whether it is in scope.
*Needs first:* comfort with **full public disclosure** of the grant and the
write-up. A company is only "case by case".
*Angle:* AI-governance verification. AISTOF has funded "verification
protocols for frontier AI training" and an AI-security accelerator.

**4. Coefficient Giving Project Tailwind: the best-fit large grant.**
*Next step:* send the expression of interest; strong candidates may be
"fast-tracked" to a pitch meeting.
*Needs first:* a founder story and an organization plan. Read the FAQ answer
on for-profits before choosing an entity, since the page was blocked here.
Expect questions on how this applies at frontier scale.
*Angle:* a new organization building **cryptographic, hardware-free
verification for AI governance**: proofs of which weights ran and what they
output, privacy-preserving audits. This matches their technical-governance
language ("privacy-preserving transparency mechanisms"). Lead with the
catastrophic-risk link or the automatic filter drops it.

**5. EA Funds Transformative AI Fund: steady and small.**
*Next step:* apply via the Paperform on the fund page for $50k-$150k
(about 6-12 months of runway plus a GPU and audit budget).
*Needs first:* nothing; individuals are eligible. For-profits are not
mentioned, so apply as an individual.
*Angle:* a "demonstration project" for verifiable AI governance.

**6. SFF rolling application: large but slow.**
*Next step:* submit the rolling application now. It automatically requests a
Speculation Grant and entry into the next round, so it is first in line when
the 2027 round opens.
*Needs first:* a **fiscal sponsor** (as an individual) or an **incorporated
company with a company bank account**. SFF may give a for-profit an
*investment* rather than a grant.
*Angle:* Main track (AI governance verification) or Freedom track
(privacy-preserving technology).

Watch-list, not top 6: **EF ESP** (strong ZK fit; ask in Office Hours to be
alerted when a privacy or ZK RFP opens); **Kusama ZK** (fast 2-4 week
review and a hash/lattice category, but needs a Kusama verifier
integration); **Mozilla Human-Scale AI** (20 Nov; needs live users and may
rule out Llama-class models); **Safe Online** (best child-safety fit;
needs an entity; wait for the next open call); **Base Builder Grants**
(small; get nominated once the app is live on Base).

## 4. Conflicts and terms to settle before applying

- **Open-source mandates:** Foresight (code, data and outputs), EF ESP (all
  outputs), Kusama, Aleo, NLnet. If the GPU prover is the company's core IP,
  scope these grants to a separable public piece (verifier, spec,
  benchmarks, audit artefacts). Otherwise accept an open-core model before
  any VC conversation.
- **Public disclosure:** Manifund publishes the grantee, amount and
  write-up. OpenAI's grant terms say not to send confidential information.
- **Charitable-purpose limits:** Lightcone's fiscal sponsorship for
  individuals, Mozilla ("charitable purpose"; for-profit budgets "narrowly
  tailored") and SFF's sponsor route all restrict what grant money can pay
  for, and can bind the funded IP to public-benefit use.
- **Grant vs. equity:** SFF can fund a for-profit as an *investment*. Base
  Batches and the Base Ecosystem Fund are equity (investor channel). Check
  SAFE or warrant terms against a later VC round.
- **Individual-to-company transition:** grants taken as an individual
  before incorporating need a clean IP assignment to the later company, and
  any open-source condition stays attached to that work. Decide the entity
  question before the large applications (Tailwind, SFF).
- **Token payouts:** ETH, DOT, STRK and ZEC grants carry price risk, KYC
  and US tax (grant income is taxable to an individual).
- **Pitch mismatch:** AI-safety funders filter for catastrophic-risk
  relevance, so lead with governance verification. Child-safety funders want
  CSEA-specific outcomes and a legal entity. ZK foundations want chain or
  ecosystem integration.
- **Claims discipline:** describe soundness (~97-bit), the privacy status
  (accepted internally, no external audit) and the Base anchoring (testnet)
  exactly as in `docs/zkmatrix-poc/ZK60_DECISIONS.md`. Overclaiming to a
  funder is a reputational risk.
